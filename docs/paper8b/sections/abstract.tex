\begin{abstract}
The companion paper~\citep{lasser2026paper8a} proves the
Aggregate B-to-C Lower Bound theorem: voluntary trade events
in a capability collective carry revealed-preference
inequalities that compose into a lower bound on realized
capability volume $\volR$.  The theorem's Part~A requires that
events be \emph{above-sufficiency} and
\emph{assumption-admissible} (S1 holds, the trade is a free
choice with non-degrading alternatives).  This paper supplies
the structural architecture that meets those preconditions and
the diagnostic layers that interpret the bound's output.

We introduce the \emph{need-sufficiency architecture}: a
poset-construction precondition that distinguishes involuntary
need-satisfaction (where sacrifice magnitude reveals
cost-of-access) from voluntary want-pursuit (where sacrifice
reveals preference strength).  Below-sufficiency sacrifices are
structurally involuntary and filtered by the free-choice
assumption (S1) before the observation channel operates.  The
sufficiency threshold establishes a \emph{polarity boundary}
for the sacrifice signal; the polarity-correct benchmark
inverts cost dimensions so that $\volL$-maximization aligns
with the agent's interest on both sides of the threshold; a
multiplicative downstream-gate on $\volL$-aggregation makes
need-satisfaction a structural precondition for registering
downstream capability weight.  A parallel \emph{need-access
cost} diagnostic tracks the benchmarked capability surrendered
for need-satisfaction (companion metric to $\betaL$, separate
from the lower bound).

We then supply the diagnostic layers that interpret the
measurement theorem's output.  The \emph{gap decomposition}
partitions the B-to-C gap $1 - \betaL$ into four disjoint
cells---restricted, covered, dormant, residual---each with a
well-defined $\delta$ term, under a poset-boundary closure
hypothesis.  The decomposition is computable in $O(|P| + E +
M)$ time from observable inputs.  A \emph{wireheading-consistent
concentration signal} derived from trade-flow
Herfindahl--Hirschman Index (Schur-convex in the majorization
order) provides a governance-thresholded alarm, with a Part-A
category-flow variant third-party observable from public
committed events.  Finally, we re-characterize the
\emph{residual class}: the set of capabilities structurally
outside the sacrifice channel's reach.  This revealed-observation
carve differs from---and is more useful than---the classical
benchmarkability-based residual class, yielding the irreducible
component of the gap decomposition.

A four-phase worked example---a two-substrate collective
traced through healthy trade, automation-driven concentration,
diagnosis and recovery, and complementary-diagnostic
cross-checking---demonstrates the architecture end-to-end.
\end{abstract}

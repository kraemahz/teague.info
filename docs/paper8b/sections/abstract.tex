\begin{abstract}
The companion paper~\citep[Revealed~Sacrifice]{lasser2026paper8a} proves the
    \emph{Aggregate B-to-C Lower Bound} theorem for a privacy-respecting
    observation channel on realized capability volume $\volR$, under
    admissibility conditions including S1 (free choice with non-degrading
    alternatives).  This paper supplies the structural condition that makes
    S1's below-sufficiency failure mode diagnosable (the actual filtering
    remains an institutional-attestation step; the condition identifies which
    events to filter, not by itself a filtering computation), together with the
    diagnostic architecture that interprets the bound's output.

The \emph{need-sufficiency architecture} is a poset-construction precondition
    distinguishing involuntary need-satisfaction from voluntary want-pursuit
    via multi-dimensional sufficiency thresholds and downstream-safe gates.  We
    establish a sufficient condition under which retention degrades the agent
    below threshold and no third non-degrading alternative exists (S1 fails),
    filtering such events from the aggregate. A parallel \emph{need-access cost}
    diagnostic tracks below-sufficiency expenditure as a companion metric.

The \emph{gap decomposition} partitions the B-to-C gap into five cells
    (restricted, covered, dormant, residual, boundary-residual) under the
    default literal-partition variant. Cell classification is computable in
    near-linear $\widetilde{O}(|P|+E+M)$ time given external admissibility
    labels and governance inputs.  A \emph{wireheading-consistent concentration
    signal} from the trade-flow Herfindahl--Hirschman Index is Schur-convex in
    the majorization order; the Part-A category-flow variant is third-party
    observable from public committed events plus the public
    S1-admissibility labelling.  The \emph{residual class} is
    re-characterized from a measurement-apparatus question to a channel-reach
    question. A worked example demonstrates the architecture end-to-end.
\end{abstract}

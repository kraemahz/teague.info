\section{Integration: Architecture Complements the Measurement
Theorem}
\label{sec:integration}

\subsection{Position in the GFM sequence}

The companion paper~\citep{lasser2026paper8a} contributes the
measurement theorem---a constructive lower bound on $\volR$
from voluntary trade events.  This paper contributes the
architectural layers that make that theorem \emph{operational}:
the filter that keeps its admissibility hypotheses satisfied
(need-sufficiency), the decomposition that interprets its
output ($\delta$-partition of the gap), the concentration-based
diagnostic that catches one specific failure mode (wireheading
HHI), and the characterization of what the measurement cannot
reach (residual class).

The pair fits the preemptive-restriction
criterion~\citep[Proposition~1]{lasser2026horizon} as follows.
Controlled relaxation~\citep{lasser2026wamura} tests the risk
dimension; revealed sacrifice (companion paper) tests the value
dimension.  When a restriction decision is pending, controlled
relaxation asks \emph{is the feared harm real?} and the
measurement theorem asks \emph{is the forgone capability
valuable enough to recover?}  The present paper adds: \emph{how
should we interpret the measurement when it fires an alarm, and
what is it structurally unable to see?}

\subsection{Connection to the Goodhart mechanism}

The need-sufficiency architecture
(Section~\ref{sec:need_sufficiency}) gives a concrete mechanism
for the kind of proxy failure studied in the Goodhart
literature~\citep{strathern1997improving,manheim2019categorizing}.
A framework that fails to distinguish needs from wants---or
fails to invert cost-dimension benchmarks---produces a proxy
$\volL$ that is systematically misaligned with $\volR$ on need
dimensions: rewarding agents for having expensive-to-satisfy
needs (high cost = high sacrifice = high ``revealed value''
under a naive reading) when the correct interpretation is the
opposite.

The polarity boundary (Definition~\ref{def:polarity_boundary})
is the framework's prophylactic against this specific Goodhart
mechanism.  Establishing the boundary and filtering
below-sufficiency sacrifices via S1 \emph{before} the sacrifice
channel operates prevents need-cost inflation from corrupting
the $\volR$ lower bound.  The $\NAC$ diagnostic
(Definition~\ref{def:nac}) tracks the excluded need-costs
separately, making the Goodhart pressure visible rather than
hidden.

\subsection{Connection to the capability approach}

The below-sufficiency / above-sufficiency partition recovers
the distinction between basic capabilities and complex
capabilities of~\citet{sen1999development}
and~\citet{nussbaum2011creating} within the single-poset
treatment.  The framework adds three formal instruments the
philosophical tradition lacks: (i)~the downstream-safety
condition
(Definition~\ref{def:downstream_safe}), which makes
``sufficient'' structurally rigorous rather than normatively
asserted; (ii)~the polarity-correct benchmark discipline
(Definition~\ref{def:polarity_benchmark}), which aligns
$\volL$-maximization with the agent's interest on both sides of
the threshold; and (iii)~the revealed-sacrifice transition
(mechanism~(c), Section~\ref{sec:threshold_setting}), which
identifies the sufficiency threshold empirically.

A fuller treatment of the correspondence between GFM and the
Sen--Nussbaum capability approach, including a formal
fungibility-collapse theorem reducing GFM to standard
microeconomics as a special case, is the subject of a companion
paper in preparation.

\subsection{The shared privacy discipline}

The present paper inherits the privacy discipline established
in the companion paper and the broader GFM sequence:
\emph{resolution through frequency, not through depth}.  The
gap-decomposition computability
(Proposition~\ref{prop:gap_computable}) operates in $O(|P| + E
+ M)$ using only public inputs (capability census, poset
structure, admissibility labels, closure policy); the
wireheading-consistent signal
(Proposition~\ref{prop:third_party_wireheading}) is third-party
observable from public committed trade data.  Neither
diagnostic requires deeper access to individual agent state
than the companion paper's measurement channel already
permits.

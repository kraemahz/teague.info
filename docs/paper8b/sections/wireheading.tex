\section{Wireheading-Consistent Signature via Trade-Flow
Concentration}
\label{sec:wireheading}

Wireheading produces high $\betaL$ with degenerate exercise: the
collective exercises capabilities, but only in narrow
self-reinforcing loops.  Under the sacrifice-based observation
model, the wireheading signature appears as \emph{trade-flow
concentration}: the collective's sacrifice events cluster in a
narrow category of unbenchmarked bundles, rather than distributing
across the capability space.

\begin{definition}[Trade-Flow Leverage]
\label{def:trade_leverage}
For bundle category $C_j \in \mathcal{C}$
(the category partition~\citep{lasser2026paper8a}), the trade-flow
leverage of $C_j$ at time $T$ is:
\[
  \lambda_{\mathrm{trade}}(j, T) \;=\;
  \begin{cases}
    \volRlower(C_j, T) \;/\; \volRlower(G, T)
      & \text{if } \volRlower(G, T) > 0, \\
    \text{undefined (diagnostic suppressed)}
      & \text{if } \volRlower(G, T) = 0.
  \end{cases}
\]
Under healthy trade diversity, leverage is distributed across many
categories.  Under wireheading, leverage concentrates on the loop
drivers.  When total observed trade flow is zero (the agent has
produced no theorem-admissible sacrifice events in the window),
$\lambda_{\mathrm{trade}}$ is not a meaningful quantity and the
diagnostic is suppressed; the relevant signal in that regime is
the gap decomposition (\S\ref{sec:gap_decomposition}), which
classifies capabilities as dormant or residual rather than as
concentration-anomalies.
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Wireheading-Consistent Concentration
Signal]
\label{prop:wireheading_hhi}
When $\volRlower(G, T) > 0$, the Herfindahl--Hirschman
Index~\citep{rhoades1993herfindahl}
\[
  \HHI(\lambda_{\mathrm{trade}}) \;=\;
  \sum_{j} \lambda_{\mathrm{trade}}(j)^2
\]
is \emph{Schur-convex} on the simplex of probability vectors:
for leverage distributions $\lambda, \lambda'$ over the same
support, if $\lambda$ majorizes $\lambda'$ (i.e., $\lambda$ is
at least as concentrated as $\lambda'$ in the sense
of~\citet{hardy1929majorization}), then $\HHI(\lambda) \geq
\HHI(\lambda')$.  Concentration in the majorization order
therefore induces a monotone ordering of $\HHI$ values, and
comparing $\HHI$ against a governance-set threshold
$\HHI_{\mathrm{alarm}}$ provides a
concentration-consistent-with-wireheading alarm: if
$\HHI > \HHI_{\mathrm{alarm}}$, a small number of bundle
categories dominate the aggregate, which is the behavioural
signature of narrow self-reinforcing exercise.
\end{proposition}

\begin{remark}[$\HHI_{\mathrm{alarm}}$ is a governance parameter,
not a theorem-derived separator]
\label{rem:hhi_alarm_governance}
The alarm threshold $\HHI_{\mathrm{alarm}}$ is not derived from
the framework's axioms; it is a governance parameter calibrated
to the framework's tolerance for concentration risk, analogous
to $\HHI$ thresholds in antitrust practice and to the sufficiency
thresholds of Definition~\ref{def:need_bundle}.  Its appropriate
value depends on the support size (number of categories with
non-zero leverage), the domain's expected baseline concentration,
and the framework's false-positive/false-negative tolerance.
The structural guarantee is that $\HHI$ is monotone in
concentration; the separation between wireheading-consistent and
wireheading-inconsistent patterns is operational, not
theorem-derived.
\end{remark}

\begin{proof}
Let $m = |\{j : \volRlower(C_j) > 0\}|$ be the number of bundle
categories with non-zero observed leverage.  The leverage vector
$\lambda = (\lambda_{\mathrm{trade}}(j))_j$ is a probability
distribution: $\lambda_j \geq 0$ and $\sum_j \lambda_j = 1$ by
the normalization in Definition~\ref{def:trade_leverage}.

\emph{Schur-convexity.}
$\HHI(\lambda) = \sum_j \lambda_j^2$ is a sum of convex functions
of the coordinates and is symmetric under permutation.  Any
symmetric convex function on the simplex is Schur-convex by the
Hardy--Littlewood--P\'olya
theorem~\citep{hardy1929majorization,marshall2011inequalities};
therefore $\lambda$ majorizing $\lambda'$ implies $\HHI(\lambda)
\geq \HHI(\lambda')$.  Equivalently, spreading mass from heavier
bins to lighter bins weakly decreases $\HHI$, and concentrating
mass from lighter bins to heavier bins weakly increases it.

\emph{Quantitative bounds.}
For any probability distribution $\lambda$ with support of size
$m$, Cauchy--Schwarz (or Jensen's inequality on the squaring
map) gives $\HHI(\lambda) \geq 1/m$, with equality iff $\lambda$
is uniform---the minimally-concentrated distribution in the
majorization order.  At the other extreme, if the top $k$
categories capture fraction $\rho$ of total leverage,
$\HHI \geq \rho^2/k$ (achieved by concentrating the top-$k$ mass
uniformly on those $k$ bins).

\emph{Alarm criterion.}
A wireheading signature concentrates trade flow in $k$
loop-driver categories with $\rho$ close to $1$ and $k$ small,
forcing $\HHI \geq \rho^2/k$.  A governance-chosen threshold
$\HHI_{\mathrm{alarm}} \in (1/m, 1)$ determines which
concentration levels trigger the alarm; by Schur-convexity, the
alarm fires on a majorization-upper set of distributions (if
$\lambda$ triggers the alarm and $\lambda' \succeq \lambda$ in
the majorization order, then $\lambda'$ also triggers it).
Whether any specific $\HHI_{\mathrm{alarm}}$ cleanly
\emph{separates} ``diverse'' from ``loop-dominated'' depends on
the domain's baseline concentration and the framework's
tolerance for false positives/negatives---the threshold is a
governance parameter, not a theorem-derived separator (see
Remark~\ref{rem:hhi_alarm_governance}).
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Advantage over structural counterfactual]
A direct-measurement approach would try to identify wireheading
via exercise-leverage concentration, requiring the marginal
$\volR$ contribution of each capability (a counterfactual query).
The trade-flow formulation surfaces the concentration signal via
$\HHI$ on observed trade data, requiring no counterfactual
computation.  The diagnostic is strictly more tractable; it
remains a signal rather than a definitive detection, per
Remark~\ref{rem:hhi_alarm_governance}.
\end{remark}

\begin{proposition}[Third-Party Observability of the
Concentration Signal (Part~A)]
\label{prop:third_party_wireheading}
When the total observed sacrifice magnitude
$\sum_n \Delta\volL(X_n) > 0$, the Part~A \emph{category-flow}
concentration statistic $\widetilde\HHI$ is computable from
public trade data (committed sacrifice events,
the committed event definition~\citep{lasser2026paper8a}) without access to any
agent's private state.  The Part~A statistic is built on the
\emph{aggregate sacrifice mass} $\Delta\volL(X_n)$, not on the
Part~B per-capability leverage $\volRlower(C_j, T)$ of
Definition~\ref{def:trade_leverage}; the two coincide only when
the per-event Part~A bound $\volR(Y_n) \geq \Delta\volL(X_n)$ is
exactly proportional to the per-category Part~B aggregate, which
is not generally the case (Part~B requires within-bundle
poset-independence and the additional decomposition assumptions
S3 and S5).  $\widetilde\HHI$ is therefore a sacrifice-mass
concentration statistic that admits the
wireheading-consistent reading of
Proposition~\ref{prop:wireheading_hhi} via the same
Schur-convexity argument applied to a different normalised
distribution; it is not a public computation of the Part~B
$\HHI$.  Specifically, define
\[
  \tilde\lambda_{\mathrm{trade}}(j, T) \;=\;
  \begin{cases}
    \dfrac{\sum_{n :\, \catYn{n} = j} \Delta\volL(X_n)}
         {\sum_n \Delta\volL(X_n)}
      & \text{if } \sum_n \Delta\volL(X_n) > 0, \\
    \text{undefined (diagnostic suppressed)}
      & \text{if } \sum_n \Delta\volL(X_n) = 0,
  \end{cases}
  \qquad
  \widetilde\HHI \;=\; \sum_j \tilde\lambda_{\mathrm{trade}}(j)^2,
\]
for the trade window $T$ (with $\widetilde\HHI$ correspondingly
suppressed when $\tilde\lambda_{\mathrm{trade}}$ is undefined).
A third-party auditor with access only to the committed
sacrifice ledger can compute $\widetilde\HHI$ whenever the
window contains positive-magnitude events and raise the Part~A
category-flow alarm (wireheading-consistent in the sense of
Proposition~\ref{prop:wireheading_hhi}, with
$\tilde\lambda_{\mathrm{trade}}$ substituted for
$\lambda_{\mathrm{trade}}$; not a wireheading determination on
its own).  When the window has zero total sacrifice flow, the
diagnostic is suppressed (the relevant signal in that regime is
the gap decomposition, \S\ref{sec:gap_decomposition}, matching
the parallel suppression rule of
Definition~\ref{def:trade_leverage}).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
By the committed event definition~\citep{lasser2026paper8a}, every committed
sacrifice event exposes the pair $(\catYn{n}, \Delta\volL(X_n))$
in cleartext.  For the category-flow statistic, both the
numerator $\sum_{n :\, \catYn{n} = j} \Delta\volL(X_n)$ (which
sums only committed fields conditional on the committed category
label) and the denominator $\sum_n \Delta\volL(X_n)$ are direct
functions of the revealed-cleartext fields.  No hidden
field---agent identity, bundle-interior subset, $\alpha_{n,c}$
coefficients---enters the computation.  Hence
$\widetilde\HHI = \sum_j \tilde\lambda_{\mathrm{trade}}(j)^2$ is
publicly computable.  The alarm test
$\widetilde\HHI > \HHI_{\mathrm{alarm}}$ is consequently
computable by any party with read access to the committed-event
ledger.  Monotonicity of $\widetilde\HHI$ in trade-flow
concentration across categories follows from the same
Cauchy--Schwarz / Herfindahl argument as
Proposition~\ref{prop:wireheading_hhi}, applied to the
category-flow distribution.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Part~B observability requires richer commitments]
\label{rem:wireheading_part_b_obs}
The per-capability Part~B concentration statistic
$\HHI(\lambda_{\mathrm{trade}})$ built on
$\volRlower(C_j, T) = \sum_{c \in C_j \cap U} \volRlower(c, T)$
is \emph{not} third-party computable from the committed-event
interface of the committed event definition~\citep{lasser2026paper8a}.  The
committed form reveals only the partition label $\catYn{n}$ and
attests $Y_n \subseteq C_{\catYn{n}}$; it does not reveal which
subset of $C_{\catYn{n}}$ was actually acquired, nor the
per-capability attribution coefficients $\alpha_{n,c}$.
Computing $\volRlower(c, T) =
\max_{n :\, c \in Y_n} \alpha_{n,c} \cdot \Delta\volL(X_n)$
therefore requires additional committed fields: per-event
subset-membership openings (or a ZK attestation equivalent), and
the $\alpha_{n,c}$ coefficients (public by an equal-weight
default, or committed alongside the event under a richer
Part~B variant as flagged in the Part~B commitment remark~\citep{lasser2026paper8a}).
Under that richer commitment, the Part~B statistic becomes
third-party observable; under the default Part~A commitment, it
is not.  Within a consented operational perimeter~$O$
(the exercise-indicator appendix of~\citep{lasser2026paper8a}), no richer commitment is
needed because direct per-capability evaluation is permitted;
the Part~B statistic is then available to the perimeter
operator, not to arbitrary third parties.
\end{remark}

\section{Integration: Duality and the Shared Privacy Discipline}
\label{sec:integration}

\subsection{The complementary pair}

\begin{center}
\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}lll@{}}
\toprule
& \textbf{Controlled relaxation}~\citep{lasser2026wamura}
& \textbf{Revealed sacrifice} (this paper) \\
\midrule
Question & Is the restriction warranted?
& Is the capability valuable? \\
Evidence & Framework-initiated exercise
& Agent-initiated sacrifice \\
Direction & Framework injects observation
& Framework receives disclosure \\
Scope & Bounded sub-poset $\scope$, bounded $\tau$
& Bounded to the trade event \\
Privacy cost & Scope-limited monitoring
& Magnitude/category disclosure (default), price-invertibility
through the public benchmark \\
Adversarial model & Agent must tolerate test
& Agent must operate (buy, work) \\
Dimension & Risk (restriction lattice)
& Value (experiential $\volR$) \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

Together they cover both axes of the preemptive-restriction
criterion of~\citep[Proposition~1]{lasser2026horizon}:
restriction cost (controlled relaxation tests the risk side)
and experiential value (revealed sacrifice bounds the value
side).  Controlled relaxation tells us whether a feared harm is
real; revealed sacrifice tells us whether the forgone capability
is worth recovering.  Without both, the restriction decision is
underdetermined.

\subsection{The shared privacy discipline}

Three papers in the
sequence~\citep{lasser2026phase,lasser2026wamura} and the present
paper share a structural discipline: \textbf{resolution through
frequency, not through depth.}

\begin{itemize}
\item The phase boundary
  of~\citep[Theorem~2]{lasser2026phase}:
  $\rho_{\min}^{\mathrm{cross}}$ counts observation
  \emph{channels}, not depth of observation per channel.
\item Controlled relaxation~\citep{lasser2026wamura}: more
  precision on risk claims comes from running more tests
  (\citealp[Theorem~2]{lasser2026wamura}, convergence depends on
  test count), not from surveilling each test more intrusively.
\item Revealed sacrifice (this paper): more precision on
  $\volR$ comes from observing more trades
  (Propositions~\ref{prop:monotone_part_a}
  and~\ref{prop:monotone}), not from observing each trader more
  intrusively.  The commitment layer
  (Section~\ref{sec:commitment}) reduces the trade-level
  observation to the formal privacy properties of
  Theorem~\ref{thm:privacy}; the operational ``privacy-minimal''
  characterization additionally requires the institutional
  preconditions of
  Proposition~\ref{prop:privacy_institutional}.
\end{itemize}

This shared discipline is the sequence's answer to the
panopticon temptation: the framework gains measurement capability
through \emph{more independent signals}, not through
\emph{deeper access to any single signal}.  This is consonant
with the cross-substrate redundancy principle
of~\citep[Theorem~2]{lasser2026phase} and the cryptographic
opacity commitment of~\citep[Definition~6]{lasser2026exo}.

\subsection{Connection to the phase-boundary analysis}

The phase-boundary analysis of~\citep{lasser2026phase} assumes
$\volL$ accurately measures the quantity the framework
optimizes.  The present paper asks whether that assumption
holds.  When $\betaL$ is low, the phase-boundary parameters
($\rS$, $r_{\mathrm{W}}$ from~\citep[Theorem~1]{lasser2026phase})
may be miscalibrated because the actor is optimizing a proxy
($\volL$) that diverges from the true target.

A natural extension (not pursued here): extend the phase-boundary
analysis to incorporate the $\volL/\volR$ divergence.  The
effective self-correction rate $\rS^{\mathrm{eff}}$ would depend
on $\betaL$.

\subsection{Architecture layers developed in the companion
paper}
\label{sec:companion_pointer}

This paper establishes the lower-bound measurement theorem as a
self-contained result.  The companion
paper~\citep[Need~Sufficiency]{lasser2026paper8b} is a downstream work that
builds three architectural layers atop the theorem proved here:

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Operationalization of S1's non-degradation
  primitive.}
  This paper states S1 with ``below-sufficiency'' as a primitive
  (Section~\ref{sec:assumptions}) and assumes an external filter
  rejects below-sufficiency events from the aggregate.  The
  companion paper~\citep[Need~Sufficiency, Section~2]{lasser2026paper8b}
  gives one
  structural implementation of that filter via multi-dimensional
  need bundles, downstream-safe sufficiency, polarity-correct
  benchmarks, and downstream-gated weighting.
\item \textbf{Gap decomposition and diagnostic interpretation.}
  Theorem~\ref{thm:aggregate_bound} lower-bounds $\volR^{U,[W]}$
  on the observed subset; interpreting \emph{why} $\betaL$ is
  low for a given window requires partitioning the gap into
  restricted, covered, dormant, residual, and (under the default
  literal-partition strategy) boundary-residual contributions.
  The companion
  paper~\citep[Need~Sufficiency, Section~3]{lasser2026paper8b}
  supplies the five-cell decomposition (default strategy~(c),
  identity by set-disjoint M5 weight summation alone), a
  four-cell variant under cooperative-closure
  strategies~(a)/(b), and an $O(|P| + E + M)$ computability
  proposition.
\item \textbf{Wireheading-consistent signature and residual
  class.}  The companion
  paper~\citep[Need~Sufficiency, Sections~4--5]{lasser2026paper8b}
  develops a
  Herfindahl--Hirschman-Index-based concentration signal from
  trade-flow data---Schur-convex in the majorization order,
  with a Part-A category-flow variant third-party observable
  from public committed events---and characterizes the
  residual class (capabilities structurally outside the
  sacrifice channel's reach) via a revealed-observation carve.
\end{itemize}

The present paper's results stand independently of the companion
paper: the proofs do not invoke any definition developed
in~\citep[Need~Sufficiency]{lasser2026paper8b}.  Readers interested only in the
measurement theorem and its commitment composition can stop
here; readers interested in the structural filter, gap
decomposition, and wireheading-consistent diagnostic should
proceed to~\citep[Need~Sufficiency]{lasser2026paper8b}.

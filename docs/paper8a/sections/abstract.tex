\begin{abstract}
The Goal-Frontier Maximization framework's central measure $\volL$ quantifies
    capability \emph{possession}, but the experiential value it protects
    requires capability \emph{exercise} ($\volR$).  Global direct measurement
    of $\volR$ is privacy-incompatible at scale.  We introduce
    \emph{revealed-sacrifice observation}: under assumptions S0--S2, S4(a) (S1
    = free choice with non-degrading alternatives), and the genuine-exchange
    conditions of Definition~\ref{def:sacrifice_event}, each voluntary trade
    event---an agent surrendering a benchmarked $X$ for an unbenchmarked bundle
    $Y$---yields a lower bound $\volR^{[W]}(Y) \geq \Delta\volL(X)$ on $Y$'s
    exercise contribution within window~$W$.  Aggregating across events whose
    bundles are individually cooperative-closed and pairwise poset-independent
    yields the \emph{Aggregate B-to-C Lower Bound theorem}: on the observed
    subset $U$, $\volR^{U,[W]} \geq \sum_n \Delta\volL(X_n)$.  The bound is
    non-decreasing in observed events and composes with the commitment protocol
    of~\citep{lasser2026exo} via two interfaces (per-event and hiding-variant
    rollup) both with three formal privacy properties plus two named
    institutional preconditions (Proposition~\ref{prop:privacy_institutional}).
    Money and time sacrifice channels triangulate the same underlying
    substrate.  We also show that $\volR$'s mapping from capability systems is
    not strictly monotone under removal of unexercised capabilities, so
    $\volL$'s self-balancing property does not transfer to $\volR$; $\volR$ is
    a diagnostic, not an optimization target.  The need-sufficiency S1 filter,
    gap decomposition, wireheading-consistent concentration signal, and
    residual class are developed
    in~\citep[Need~Sufficiency]{lasser2026paper8b}.
\end{abstract}

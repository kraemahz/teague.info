\section{Operationalization of Conjecture 1}
\label{sec:conjecture_op}

Theorem~\ref{thm:deployment_safety} depends on \citep[Microfoundation]{lasser2026micro}'s
Conjecture~1 (optimization pressure correlates with gap exploitation)
through invariant $I_5$ (HHI ceiling) and Assumption SA1 (HHI
surrogate adequacy). This section discusses the structure of that
dependency: what HHI proves about Conjecture~1 (Lemma~3 forward
direction), what SA1 assumes (the reverse direction needed by the
theorem), what SA1 buys vs.\ what proving Conjecture~1 outright
would buy, and the empirical validation pathway deferred to
follow-up work.

\subsection{The Conjecture 1 dependency}
\label{sec:conjecture_dependency}

\citep[Microfoundation]{lasser2026micro}'s Conjecture~1 states informally: optimization pressure on
the proxy $\Pproxy = \volL$ correlates with exploitation of the
proxy-truth gap $\epsnonres$. The conjecture's implication for
deployment safety is: when the system is in the
optimization-pressure regime $\mathcal{R}_{\mathrm{press}}$, the gap
exploitation predicted by the conjecture would invalidate the
static Goodhart bound's intensivity property.

Conjecture~1 is conjectural, not proved. \citep[Microfoundation]{lasser2026micro} explicitly defers
its formal treatment, marking it ``conjectural'' in
\citep[\S goodhart]{lasser2026micro}. This paper's deployment claim
must address this: Theorem~1's Layer~1 binding requires the
deployment to be \emph{outside} $\mathcal{R}_{\mathrm{press}}$, but
we cannot assert this without invoking some characterization of
$\mathcal{R}_{\mathrm{press}}$.

The HHI ceiling $I_5$ provides such a characterization in
operational terms: deployments with $\HHI < H^*$ are claimed (via
SA1) to be outside $\mathcal{R}_{\mathrm{press}}$. The remainder of
this section discusses what this claim is, what it buys, and what
remains open.

\subsection{The HHI surrogate: what Lemma 3 actually proves}
\label{sec:hhi_lemma3}

Lemma~\ref{lem:3_forward} (\S\ref{sec:lem3}) establishes the
forward implication:
\[
  \HHI > H^* \;\Longrightarrow\;
  \text{trade-flow concentration prerequisites of }
  \mathcal{R}_{\mathrm{press}} \text{ are met}.
\]
The proof chain goes from HHI's Schur-convexity property
(\citep[Schur-convexity]{lasser2026paper8b}) through trade-flow
concentration (\citep[Revealed~Sacrifice]{lasser2026paper8a}'s exercise-pattern interpretation) to
optimization-target concentration (the structural prerequisite of
Conjecture~1's predicted-exploitation regime).

This is a one-way implication. Lemma~\ref{lem:3_forward} \emph{does
not} establish the reverse: not every deployment in
$\mathcal{R}_{\mathrm{press}}$ has $\HHI > H^*$. There may exist
deployments where optimization pressure is concentrated on the
proxy without manifesting as concentrated trade flow --- e.g., an
optimizer that distributes its proxy-pressure across many trade
categories while concentrating its internal computation on a single
proxy-targeting strategy. Lemma~\ref{lem:3_forward} does not rule
out such deployments.

\subsection{The SA1 assumption}
\label{sec:sa1}

Theorem~1 Layer~1 requires the contrapositive of
Lemma~\ref{lem:3_forward}: $\HHI < H^* \implies \mathcal{R}_{\mathrm{press}}^c$
(deployments outside the HHI ceiling are outside the pressure
regime). This is the reverse direction Lemma~\ref{lem:3_forward}
does not provide. We close the gap with explicit Assumption SA1:

\begin{quote}
\textbf{SA1 (HHI surrogate adequacy, restated from
\S\ref{sec:lem3}).}  For deployments under stance S0 with
operational invariants $I_1, \ldots, I_4$ in force, the HHI ceiling
$\HHI < H^*$ is sufficient to ensure the deployment lies outside
Conjecture~1's predicted-exploitation regime
$\mathcal{R}_{\mathrm{press}}$:
\[
  \HHI < H^* \;\Longrightarrow\; \mathcal{R}_{\mathrm{press}}^c.
\]
\end{quote}

SA1 is an \emph{empirical adequacy claim}: it asserts that, in the
class of deployments meeting (C1)--(C5) of Theorem~1, HHI is a
sufficient proxy for the absence of optimization pressure on the
proxy. SA1 is testable in principle: comparing actual gap-exploitation
behavior against HHI levels in real deployments would either
support or falsify the assumption.

\paragraph{What SA1 is, and what it is not.}

SA1 is \emph{not} a derivative of source-paper machinery. It is an
explicit assumption that the deployment claim conditions on,
analogous to (C1)--(C5) of Theorem~1. SA1's status in this paper is
identical to Conjecture~1's status in \citep[Microfoundation]{lasser2026micro}: it is named, its
empirical content is specified, and its formal proof is deferred.

SA1 is \emph{not} a weakening of Conjecture~1. The two are
complementary: Conjecture~1 says optimization pressure correlates
with gap exploitation; SA1 says HHI is a measurable surrogate for
optimization-target concentration that captures the
pressure-regime's structural prerequisites. Both could be true;
neither is yet proved.

\subsection{What SA1 buys vs.\ what proving Conjecture 1 would buy}
\label{sec:sa1_buys}

The deployment-safety literature has two structurally different
options for handling the Conjecture-1 dependency. They produce
deployment claims of different strengths.

\textbf{Option A (This paper's choice): Operationalize via SA1.}
Adopt $I_5$ (HHI ceiling) as the operational invariant and SA1 as
an explicit assumption. The deployment claim becomes: under
(C1)--(C5) and SA1, the Goodhart slack is bounded by an intensive
constant.

\emph{What this buys:}  An operationally-checkable deployment
claim. Operators monitor $I_5$ (a ledger-observable quantity); SA1
is a stated condition that operators can read and assess. The
deployment scales past any capability level the deployment's
evaluation methods can characterize, conditional on the named
assumption.

\emph{What this does not buy:}  Independence from SA1. If SA1 is
empirically false in some regime, $I_5$ is the wrong invariant and
the deployment claim is unsound for that regime.

\textbf{Option B (deferred): Prove Conjecture~1.}
Formally establish that optimization pressure on the proxy
correlates with gap exploitation under stated structural
assumptions. The deployment claim becomes: under (C1)--(C5), the
Goodhart slack is bounded by an intensive constant unconditionally
(no SA1).

\emph{What this would buy:}  Independence from SA1. The deployment
claim becomes a conditional theorem (conditional only on
(C1)--(C5)) rather than a conditional theorem with an additional
empirical adequacy assumption. The structural argument for the
HHI surrogate becomes a derived theorem rather than an assumed
relationship.

\emph{What this requires:}  Formal proof of Conjecture~1, which is
substantial open work. The conjecture's predicates (concentration,
gradient existence, computational capacity) need formal definition;
the correlation claim needs either a structural derivation from
prior axioms or an empirical validation framework that supports a
formal claim.

\paragraph{Why this paper chooses Option A.}

Three reasons:

\emph{Visibility of the empirical content.}  SA1 is what this paper
actually needs.  Asserting that $I_5$ is sufficient without naming
SA1 would bury the empirical content of the deployment claim.
Naming SA1 makes the content visible: operators see exactly what
they are conditioning on, and reviewers see exactly what the
deployment claim does and does not establish.

\emph{Operational tractability.}  Option A delivers a deployment
claim that is invokable today: operators can implement \citep[Exogenous~Verification]{lasser2026exo}'s
verification infrastructure, monitor $I_5$, and invoke Theorem~1
under stated conditions. Option B is contingent on formal work
that may take years and may not yield a formal proof at all.

\emph{Conjecture-1 follow-up is its own paper.}  Establishing
Conjecture~1 (or refuting it) is a substantial research program
with its own scope. This paper's deployment claim should not be held
hostage to that program; the conditional formulation lets this paper
ship while the conjecture program continues.

\subsection{Empirical validation pathway}
\label{sec:sa1_empirical}

SA1 is empirically testable. The validation pathway compares actual
gap-exploitation behavior against HHI levels in real deployments.
The structure of such a study:

\begin{enumerate}
\item Identify deployments meeting (C1)--(C5) of Theorem~1 (the
  precondition class for which SA1 is asserted to hold).
\item Measure HHI on trade flows continuously, computing the
  invariant $\HHI < H^*$ across rolling windows.
\item Independently measure proxy-truth gap exploitation: cases
  where the agent achieves high $\Pproxy$-score with low
  $\Ttruth$-realization, evaluated against ground-truth realizations
  on a held-out subset.
\item Test the empirical relationship: do deployments with $\HHI <
  H^*$ exhibit gap-exploitation rates significantly below
  deployments with $\HHI \geq H^*$? A statistically significant
  separation supports SA1; the absence of separation refutes it.
\end{enumerate}

The study is a candidate for follow-up empirical work. If
successful, SA1 graduates from assumption to derived consequence,
and Theorem~1's deployment claim strengthens correspondingly. If
falsified, $I_5$ is the wrong invariant and This paper's deployment
claim must be revised (either by replacing $I_5$ or by restricting
scope).

The follow-up paper proposed in \S\ref{sec:scope} (the
channel-mediation conjecture and HHI--$\Delta r_K$ structural
relationship) is the natural home for this empirical work. The
conjecture-mediation work would establish the structural connection
between trade-flow concentration and the optimization-pressure
regime; combined with the empirical validation above, it would
either prove or falsify SA1 directly.

\subsection{Conditional structure of Theorem 1}
\label{sec:conditional_structure}

Theorem~1 binds when its conditions hold.  The conditions divide
into three structurally distinct categories: operational conditions
verifiable from deployment state; conjectural conditions argued on
strong grounds but not formally proved within this paper or its
sources; and explicit scope restrictions naming what the theorem
does not cover.  The distinction matters for what an operator can
do with the theorem and where its load-bearing weight rests.

\paragraph{Operational conditions: measurement, not theorem.}
The bulk of the conditions are operationally checkable: the
operator measures, audits, calibrates, and the conditions either
hold or do not.  The theorem itself does not say anything about
whether any particular deployment satisfies them; that is a
measurement question, not a theorem question, and the deployment-
tooling section (\S\ref{sec:deployment_tooling}) specifies the
audits.
\begin{itemize}
\item (C1) Eleven operational invariants $I_1$--$I_{11}$:
  ledger-observable under the verification infrastructure.
\item (C2) Canonical tripartite substrate identification:
  deployment-design decision verified by failure-correlation
  auditing (Audit~1).
\item (C3) Cooperative-overlap regime: substrate-cooperative
  structure dominated by cross-substrate cooperatives, measured by
  the cooperative-vs-redundancy audit (Audit~3).
\item (C5) Continuous SPRT monitoring + (C5.SPRT) sub-clauses
  (HOEFF/MULT/IID/SUPP): operational monitor + calibrated constants
  ($\Bclip$, $\Kch$, $\deltastar$ post-clipping); Audit~7.
\item (C6)--(C12) (other than C4): bounded-Lipschitz alignment,
  bounded co-evolution, per-capability admissibility, world-model
  parameterization regularity, pairwise cooperative-production
  floor, bounded gap-growth rate, clock comparability, and
  environment-side witness instantiation.  Each is calibrated by a
  named audit (4 through 8); the theorem treats each as a deployment-
  class condition with $|P|$-independent constants.
\end{itemize}
The theorem's claim about these is conditional: \emph{if} they
hold, \emph{then} the bound binds.  Whether they hold in a given
deployment is what the audit infrastructure is for.

\paragraph{Conjectural conditions: argued, not proved.}
Two conditions rest on argument or restricted plausibility, not on
proof:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{(SA1) HHI surrogate adequacy.}  The forward direction
  of \citep[Microfoundation]{lasser2026micro}'s Conjecture~1 is
  structurally argued in Lemma~\ref{lem:3_forward}; the reverse
  direction --- $\HHI < H^* \implies \mathcal{R}_{\mathrm{press}}^c$,
  needed for $I_5$ to bind in Layer~1 --- is not proved by this
  paper.  SA1 names this as an explicit empirical assumption; the
  validation pathway in \S\ref{sec:sa1_empirical} lays out how SA1
  can be tested across deployment classes, and the channel-mediation
  conjecture proposed in \S\ref{sec:scope} is the natural home for
  closing this gap structurally.
\item \textbf{(C4) Inner-alignment condition.}  The agent's
  effective objective realizing causally-grounded cooperative-
  outcome value is assumed.  Vanilla RLHF does not deliver (C4);
  achieving it requires training-time discipline that is itself
  open research (\S\ref{sec:weaker_c4}).
\end{itemize}
SA1 has empirical precedent in the trade-flow concentration
literature; (C4) is the weakest inner-alignment condition
consistent with the cooperative-anchoring argument.  Neither is
formally proved; the conditional structure of the theorem is what
carries them.

\paragraph{Scope restrictions: not in the theorem's reach.}
Some classes are explicitly outside the guarantee:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Substrate-targeting scope.}  Theorem~1 covers
  substrate-targeting adversarial events.  Capability-targeting,
  coalition-internal-corruption, and environment-witness-orthogonal
  manipulations fall into residuals (R3) and (R4) and require
  separate machinery.
\item \textbf{Basin entry.}  The theorem assumes the deployment is
  in the cooperative-anchoring basin; it does not establish that a
  deployment can reach that basin from arbitrary initial conditions.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{What this means for SA1 specifically.}
If SA1 holds empirically, the deployment claim is fully
operationally checkable: every other conditional is verifiable
from ledger state.  If SA1 fails for some deployment class, that
class is outside the deployment claim's scope; operators must either
find an alternative invariant for the pressure regime or accept
that their deployment is in the residual class for which the claim
does not bind.

This paper does not claim universal safety.  It claims that under
named operational discipline plus the conjectural conditions named
above, deployment is provably safe in a precise sense.

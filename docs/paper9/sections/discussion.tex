\section{Discussion and Open Questions}
\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{What this paper establishes}
\label{sec:established}

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Quantification of the capability welfare economics
  tradition.}  The Sen 1985 capability set, the
  Sen functioning vector, the Becker household production
  function, and the SSF 2009 gap-decomposition catalogue all
  admit measure-theoretic counterparts in the GFM apparatus
  (poset, $\volL$, $\volRwin$, M6 cooperative capabilities,
  $\ResS$, the five-cell $\delta$-decomposition).  The
  Alkire--Foster~\citep{alkire2011counting} multidimensional-
  poverty methodology (the closest existing quantitative
  apparatus) is contained as a special case under additional
  structural commitments (cooperative excess via M6, polarity
  discipline).  Adjacent formal-welfare-economics work
  (\citet{bernheim2009beyond} on behavioral welfare economics,
  \citet{basu2011functionings} on prior capability-set
  formalizations, \citet{sugden2003opportunity} and
  \citet{pattanaik2000ranking} on opportunity-set axiomatics,
  \citet{adler2012wellbeing} on prioritarian SWF) is engaged in
  Section~\ref{sec:lineage_historical} as structural neighbors
  rather than as channel-bound precursors.  The positioning is analogous to the
  Bentham-to-expected-utility formalization that the
  welfare-economics literature spent roughly a
  century-and-a-half completing for utility theory.

\item \textbf{GFM strictly contains standard utility theory at the
  linear / risk-neutral case (containment result)}
  (Theorem~\ref{thm:fungibility_collapse}).  Under fungibility
  collapse (the structural reduction in which the capability
  poset degenerates to a single purchasing-power dimension,
  cooperative capabilities vanish, and individuation becomes
  irrelevant), an actor maximizing $\volL$ on $\Pstd$ is
  observationally equivalent to a linear-utility expected-utility
  maximizer.  Together with the five-features catalog of
  Section~\ref{sec:fc_features}, this establishes containment
  (utility theory $\subset$ GFM at $\Pstd$) plus extension (GFM
  has structural content beyond $\Pstd$ that utility theory
  lacks).  Concavity, risk-aversion, and heterogeneous utility
  recover through additional calibrations.

\item \textbf{Falsifiable structural-prediction class}
  (Section~\ref{sec:structural_predictions}).  Five predictions
  (bundle-completion magnitude, cooperative premium, saturation
  transition, population-scale anti-monopolar, mixed-polarity
  bundle-for-bundle) that utility theory either does not make,
  or can match only by adding ad hoc unfalsifiable structure.
  The predictions are what becomes falsifiable when the
  qualitative tradition is quantified.

\item \textbf{Goodhart theorem for proxy-instrumental alignment}
  (Theorem~\ref{thm:goodhart}).  Each Lipschitz alignment
  property in the GFM sequence, viewed as a function of the
  proxy $\volL$, deviates from its value under the operational
  truth $T = \volRwin$ (under~\citep[Revealed~Sacrifice]{lasser2026paper8a}'s
  S0) by at most $\mathrm{Lip}(g) \cdot \epsnonres$.
  Optimization pressure correlates with gap-exploitation as a
  qualitative direction; the wireheading-consistent HHI
  of~\citep[Need~Sufficiency]{lasser2026paper8b} is the
  deployment-readable empirical witness.

\item \textbf{Better economic models tighten alignment, in four
  operationally distinct channels}
  (Corollary~\ref{cor:direction},
  Proposition~\ref{prop:channel_composition}).  Conditional on
  per-capability admissibility
  (Assumption~\ref{ass:per_cap_admissibility}), the four
  channels (observation density (Channel~1), attestation
  quality (Channel~2), individuation discipline (Channel~3),
  and bundle decomposition (Channel~4)) each deliver a
  reduction to a non-overlapping component of the
  alignment-slack diagnostic.  Channel~3 contributes through the
  $\lambda$-weighted net
  $\Delta_3^{\lambda} = \lambda \cdot \Delta_3^{\mathrm{floor}} -
  \Delta_3^{\mathrm{new}}$, capturing both the floor reduction
  from re-individuation and the new within-channel ceiling
  contribution from the newly-individuated capability.  The
  channels compose into an aggregate alignment-slack reduction
  bounded above by the sum
  $(\Delta_1 + \Delta_2 + \Delta_4) + \Delta_3^{\lambda}$,
  capped at the pre-intervention slack.  Each channel formalizes
  a move the qualitative tradition has been negotiating
  informally.

\item \textbf{Residual-class floor as a shared problem with
  welfare economics.}  $\epsfloor$ is non-zero in any
  deployment with non-empty $\ResS$, and it is not unique to
  GFM: the shadow-economy / non-market-activity gap of standard
  welfare economics~\citep{stiglitz2009report} is the same
  structural object viewed from a different side.  Imputation
  methods (imputed rent, time-use surveys, satellite accounts)
  are the existing partial answer; in GFM terms these are
  Channel~3 re-individuation moves.  Alignment-tightening is
  bounded below by $\lambda \cdot \epsfloor$ regardless of how
  much within-channel observation infrastructure the deployment
  invests in.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{What this paper defers}
\label{sec:deferred}

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Per-property Lipschitz constants and the
  perturbative roadmap.}
  Theorem~\ref{thm:goodhart} is conditional on $g$ being
  Lipschitz on the relevant capability subspace with a
  property-specific $\mathrm{Lip}(g)$.  The perturbative
  roadmap of Section~\ref{sec:goodhart_examples} identifies
  five candidate alignment properties (anti-monopolar
  threshold, phase-boundary location, convergence rate, damage
  bound, aggregate-lower-bound slack) whose Lipschitz analyses
  would close the per-property bounds.  Each requires its own
  perturbation analysis, which is outside this paper's scope.

\item \textbf{Phase-boundary comparison theorem under variable
  proxy.}  Showing the self-correcting basin
  of~\citep[Phase~Redundancy]{lasser2026phase} grows
  monotonically as $\epsnonres \to 0$ requires a comparison
  theorem on coupled Lyapunov functions with different
  reference measures.  This is the hardest single piece in the
  perturbative roadmap and may require new mathematical
  machinery; Open~Question~\ref{oq:phase_boundary} states the
  question precisely.

\item \textbf{Empirical validation of the structural
  predictions.}
  The five predictions of Section~\ref{sec:structural_predictions}
  and the cross-domain Goodhart test of
  Section~\ref{sec:operationalizing} are testable in principle
  but not tested in this paper.  The contribution at this stage
  is to identify the predictions clearly enough that they can be
  tested and to establish the methodology template through
  which testing operates.

\item \textbf{Normative content.}  Which capabilities count as
  central, in Nussbaum's substantive
  sense~\citep{nussbaum2000women,nussbaum2011creating}, is
  outside the formal apparatus.  GFM provides the structural
  scaffold; the choice of normative loading (which
  capabilities matter, with which weights, in which
  contexts) is upstream of the formal apparatus and varies
  across deployments and societies.  The paper takes no
  position on the normative question.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Structural limitation: Channel~3 can worsen the alignment slack}
\label{sec:channel3_limitation}

This is a first-order limitation worth flagging explicitly, and
a structural one rather than a deferred-work item.  Channel~3
(individuation discipline) contributes to the alignment-slack
reduction through the $\lambda$-weighted net
$\Delta_3^{\lambda} = \lambda \cdot \Delta_3^{\mathrm{floor}}
- \Delta_3^{\mathrm{new}}$, which can be \emph{negative} when
the newly-individuated capability's within-channel ceiling
contribution exceeds $\lambda$ times the floor reduction
(Remark~\ref{rem:channel3_net}).  In that regime, a
re-individuation move that looks like ``progress'' (more of
$\ResS$ pulled into the observable subset) actually loosens the
certified alignment bound rather than tightening it.

This is not a corner case; it is a first-order concern for the
empirical programme.  Re-individuation is the move welfare
economics has been performing for decades through imputation
methods, and the current paper does not establish when
imputation is alignment-tightening versus alignment-loosening.
Concretely: a deployment that introduces a new
imputed-time-priced capability for household labour (reducing
$\delta_{\mathrm{residual}}$ by the imputed share) adds the
imputation method's measurement noise to the within-channel
ceiling.  If the imputation is noisy enough, the deployment's
$\Delta_3^{\lambda}$ is negative and the intervention worsens
the certified alignment bound.  ``Tighten all four channels''
is therefore not a blanket recommendation; deployments must
check the per-channel admissibility, and Channel~3 in
particular requires a deployment-specific imputation-quality
assessment that the apparatus does not provide.  Whether GFM's
machinery (cooperative caps, individuation discipline,
downstream cone, polarity boundary) gives sharper imputation
tools than welfare economics' standard methods is the question
of Open~Question~\ref{oq:floor}; the limitation flagged here is
that, in the absence of such tools, Channel~3 interventions
can backfire, and the paper provides no positive structural
guidance for telling when this happens.

\subsection{Open questions}
\label{sec:open_questions}

The following eight questions identify directions for
subsequent work and indicate how each would compose with the
machinery of this paper.

\paragraph{OQ 1: The phase-boundary perturbation theorem.}
\label{oq:phase_boundary}
Showing the self-correcting basin
of~\citep[Theorem~1]{lasser2026phase} grows monotonically as
$\epsnonres \to 0$ requires a comparison theorem on coupled
Lyapunov functions with different reference measures.  The
question is precise: given Lyapunov functions
$V_P$ (defined on the proxy measure $P$) and $V_T$ (defined on
the operational truth $T$), and the basin
$\mathcal{B}_P = \{x : V_P(x) \leq c\}$ for some threshold $c$,
how does $\mathcal{B}_T$ relate to $\mathcal{B}_P$ as a
function of $\epsnonres$?  The expected answer: $\mathcal{B}_T$
differs from $\mathcal{B}_P$ by a boundary layer of width
$O(\epsnonres)$ with a constant depending on the Lyapunov
functions' smoothness profiles.  Establishing the constant is
the perturbation-analysis work.  This is the hardest single
piece of the perturbative roadmap.

\paragraph{OQ 2: Non-monotonicity risk under multi-dimensional
$\epsnonres$.}
\label{oq:nonmonotonicity}
Definition~\ref{def:eps_nonres} aggregates relative deviations
across capability dimensions via a sup-norm.  This is
conservative but coarse: a deployment might tighten on some
dimensions while loosening on others, and the sup-norm captures
only the worst-case dimension.  When does multi-dimensional
$\epsnonres$ admit non-monotonic alignment-tightening?  A
weighted norm matching the safety-relevance weights $w_k$ from
the alignment property's Lyapunov function might give a
tighter characterization, but the safety-relevance weighting is
itself application-dependent.  The question is whether a
deployment-readable safety-relevance weighting can be constructed
for the channels of Section~\ref{sec:lineage}, and whether such a
weighting changes the channel composition of
Proposition~\ref{prop:channel_composition}.

\paragraph{OQ 3: The Goodhart divergence rate $f(C)$.}
\label{oq:goodhart_rate}
Conjecture~\ref{conj:optimization_pressure} is qualitative: more capable
optimizers exploit larger sets of proxy-truth divergence
regions, widening the observed $\epsnonres$ at equilibrium.  A
precise functional form $f: C \mapsto \epsnonres(C)$ such that
$\epsnonres(C) \geq f(C) \cdot \epsnonres(0)$ at equilibrium is
optimizer-architecture-dependent.  Bounds under specific
optimizer models (greedy local search, gradient-based methods,
planning-based search, learned-policy optimization) are
concrete sub-questions.  Each architecture has its own $f$, and
the structural common content (more reach $\to$ more
exploitation) is what Conjecture~\ref{conj:optimization_pressure} captures
without committing to architecture.  Closing the architecture-
specific bounds is subsequent perturbative-analysis work.

\paragraph{OQ 4: Completing the Arrow-avoidance comparison.}
\label{oq:arrow}
Section~\ref{sec:lineage_historical} argues that GFM
\emph{appears} to sidestep Arrow's original
formulation~\citep{arrow1951social} by being a measure on a
poset rather than an ordinal-preference aggregator, with
partial analogs of four of the five Arrovian axioms
(transitivity, unrestricted domain, Pareto via M3,
non-dictatorship via M5) and IIA inapplicable to a volume
measure.  Sidestepping the original formulation is not the same
as proving no analogous impossibility holds.  A complete
treatment requires (i)~a precise formulation of which
alternative impossibility results, if any, apply to volume
measures on posets, (ii)~comparison with Sen's own ways
out~\citep{sen1970collective}, and (iii)~explicit handling of
the heterogeneous-agent aggregation question that
Remark~\ref{rem:heterogeneity} flagged as a separate analytical
problem.  The lineage subsection states the partial argument;
the complete version is open.

\paragraph{OQ 5: Normative loading and alignment-target
sensitivity.}
\label{oq:normative}
GFM provides the structural scaffold; normative choices
(``which capabilities matter'') are loaded externally.  Two
deployments with different normative loadings get different
alignment guarantees from the same framework: the economics
affects the tightness of the guarantees, but the normative
loading affects the target.  The question is whether the
Lipschitz transfer of Theorem~\ref{thm:goodhart} is robust
to normative-loading variation, in the sense that the
transfer's content does not depend on the specific loading
within a structural admissibility class.  A negative answer
would mean alignment-tightening claims need to be qualified
per-loading; a positive answer would establish that the
formalization is normative-loading-invariant within an
admissibility class.

\paragraph{OQ 6: Quantitative rate for $\epsnonres$
convergence.}
\label{oq:rate}
~\citep[Propositions~2~and~3]{lasser2026paper8a} prove
$\betaL$ is non-decreasing under admissible accumulation but
do not bound how fast $\betaL$ converges.  The rate depends on
(i)~S0 attestation quality (which itself is institutional-layer-
dependent;~\citep[Remark~18]{lasser2026paper8a}),
(ii)~Part-A vs Part-B aggregation choice,
(iii)~observation density on the deployment's capability
inventory, and (iv)~the residual-class floor $\epsfloor$
itself.  None of (i)--(iv) is bounded in this paper.
Bounding the rate is the subsequent perturbative-analysis
question for the better-economics-tightens-alignment
direction; until it is bounded, Corollary~\ref{cor:direction}
is restricted to direction (monotonicity) rather than rate.
This is also \citep[Open~Question~6]{lasser2026paper8b}'s
territory.

\paragraph{OQ 7: Residual-class floor on tightening, shared with
welfare economics.}
\label{oq:floor}
$\epsfloor$ sets a structural floor below which the
within-channel gap component cannot be driven by any amount of
observation.  This floor is \emph{not unique to GFM}; it is
the formal version of the same structural measurement gap that
welfare economics has navigated for decades under names like
``shadow economy,'' ``household production,''
``non-market activity''~\citep{stiglitz2009report}.  Imputation
methods (imputed rent, time-use surveys, satellite accounts)
are the existing partial answer in welfare economics; in GFM
terms, these are Channel~3 re-individuation moves.
Characterizing what governs the floor (individuation
discipline, governance choices about ``no sacrifice-observable
path in principle''), and whether GFM's apparatus (cooperative
caps, individuation discipline, downstream cone, polarity
boundary) gives sharper imputation tools than the standard
methods, is open.  This question is structurally distinct from
OQ~6: OQ~6 asks how fast the within-channel gap closes; OQ~7
asks how low the floor goes.  OQ~7 is shared with welfare
economics rather than unique to the alignment programme.

\paragraph{OQ 8: Adversarial degradation of attestation under
optimization pressure.}
\label{oq:adversarial_attestation}
\citep[Revealed~Sacrifice]{lasser2026paper8a}'s S0
(calibration), S1 (free choice), and S4(a) (valuation
additivity) are not zero-knowledge-verifiable under the default
commitment scheme~\citep[Remark~18]{lasser2026paper8a}; they
require institutional attestation.  The
better-economics-tightens-alignment direction
(Corollary~\ref{cor:direction}) depends on attestation
\emph{quality}: the framework can drive $\epsnonres$ downward
only when the attestation layer reliably classifies events
under S0, S1, and S4(a).  But the same optimization pressure
that widens $P$-$T$ divergence regions
(Conjecture~\ref{conj:optimization_pressure}) also incentivizes adversarial
degradation of the attestation layer itself: a sufficiently
capable optimizer that finds proxy-truth divergence cheaper to
exploit than proxy-truth alignment will \emph{also} find
attestation manipulation cheaper than honest attestation,
given any institutional layer that admits adversarial pressure.
Bounding the attestation layer's adversarial robustness (sybil
resistance, collusion resistance, dispute-resolution adequacy
under optimizer-controlled inputs) is essential to the
operational substance of Corollary~\ref{cor:direction}.  This
is the natural follow-up to~\citep[Revealed~Sacrifice]{lasser2026paper8a}'s
external-attestation-protocol scope-and-follow-up note, but
with the Goodhart-theorem framing: how does attestation
quality scale under adversarial pressure, and what
incentive-compatibility properties does the layer need to
deliver reliable $\epsnonres$ reduction in the optimizer-pressure
regime?  OQ~8 is the natural counterpart on the attestation side
to OQ~3 on the optimizer side.

\subsection{Reading the paper across audiences}
\label{sec:audience}

The paper is written partly for alignment researchers and partly
for welfare economists, with the through-line connecting both
audiences: the alignment programme of the GFM sequence is the
formalization-payoff of the welfare-economics tradition's
forty-year qualitative negotiation, and progress in either
discipline drives the same $\epsnonres$ that the other depends
on.  Some duplication of background is unavoidable, and we have
tried to keep technical machinery in clearly delimited
sections (the Goodhart theorem and its proof in
Section~\ref{sec:goodhart_theorem}, the channel composition in
Section~\ref{sec:channel_composition}) so each audience can
navigate to the parts most relevant to their starting context.

For alignment researchers, the load-bearing content is
Theorem~\ref{thm:goodhart} together with
Proposition~\ref{prop:channel_composition}: the proxy-truth
gap is structurally controllable through four operationally
distinct interventions, each tied to a specific component of
the gap decomposition, and each composing into a specific
alignment-tightening consequence.  The capability-economics
lineage of Section~\ref{sec:lineage_historical} is the
historical-confirmation evidence that the channels are real
welfare-economics moves rather than apparatus-internal
inventions.

For welfare economists, the load-bearing content is the
quantification thesis of
Section~\ref{sec:quantification_thesis} together with the
five-features catalogue of Section~\ref{sec:fc_features}: GFM
is a measure-theoretic formalization of the Sen--Nussbaum
tradition, with axiomatic structure that constrains where
Becker's flexible household-production functions become
predictively sharp and that gives the SSF~2009 gap a
deployment-readable cell decomposition.  The Goodhart theorem
is then a result of independent welfare-economics interest:
it bounds the cost of optimizing a coarse welfare proxy
against a finer operational truth, with the cost decomposing
across the same imputation channels welfare economics already
uses.

\section{Wireheading-Consistent Signature via Trade-Flow
Concentration}
\label{sec:wireheading}

Wireheading produces high $\betaL$ with degenerate exercise: the
collective exercises capabilities, but only in narrow
self-reinforcing loops.  Under the sacrifice-based observation
model, the wireheading signature appears as \emph{trade-flow
concentration}: the collective's sacrifice events cluster in a
narrow category of unbenchmarked bundles, rather than distributing
across the capability space.

\begin{definition}[Trade-Flow Leverage]
\label{def:trade_leverage}
For bundle category $C_j \in \mathcal{C}$
(Definition~\ref{def:category_partition}), the trade-flow
leverage of $C_j$ at time $T$ is:
\[
  \lambda_{\mathrm{trade}}(j, T) \;=\;
  \begin{cases}
    \volRlower(C_j, T) \;/\; \volRlower(G, T)
      & \text{if } \volRlower(G, T) > 0, \\
    \text{undefined (diagnostic suppressed)}
      & \text{if } \volRlower(G, T) = 0.
  \end{cases}
\]
Under healthy trade diversity, leverage is distributed across many
categories.  Under wireheading, leverage concentrates on the loop
drivers.  When total observed trade flow is zero (the agent has
produced no theorem-admissible sacrifice events in the window),
$\lambda_{\mathrm{trade}}$ is not a meaningful quantity and the
diagnostic is suppressed; the relevant signal in that regime is
the gap decomposition (\S\ref{sec:gap_decomposition}), which
classifies capabilities as dormant or residual rather than as
concentration-anomalies.
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Wireheading-Consistent Concentration
Signal]
\label{prop:wireheading_hhi}
When $\volRlower(G, T) > 0$, the Herfindahl--Hirschman
Index~\citep{rhoades1993herfindahl}
\[
  \HHI(\lambda_{\mathrm{trade}}) \;=\;
  \sum_{j} \lambda_{\mathrm{trade}}(j)^2
\]
is monotone in the concentration of observed trade flow.
Comparing $\HHI$ against a governance-set threshold
$\HHI_{\mathrm{alarm}}$ therefore provides a
concentration-consistent-with-wireheading alarm: if
$\HHI > \HHI_{\mathrm{alarm}}$, a small number of bundle
categories dominate the aggregate, which is the behavioural
signature of narrow self-reinforcing exercise.
\end{proposition}

\begin{remark}[$\HHI_{\mathrm{alarm}}$ is a governance parameter,
not a theorem-derived separator]
\label{rem:hhi_alarm_governance}
The alarm threshold $\HHI_{\mathrm{alarm}}$ is not derived from
the framework's axioms; it is a governance parameter calibrated
to the framework's tolerance for concentration risk, analogous
to $\HHI$ thresholds in antitrust practice and to the sufficiency
thresholds of Definition~\ref{def:need_bundle}.  Its appropriate
value depends on the support size (number of categories with
non-zero leverage), the domain's expected baseline concentration,
and the framework's false-positive/false-negative tolerance.
The structural guarantee is that $\HHI$ is monotone in
concentration; the separation between wireheading-consistent and
wireheading-inconsistent patterns is operational, not
theorem-derived.
\end{remark}

\begin{proof}
Let $m = |\{j : \volRlower(C_j) > 0\}|$ be the number of bundle
categories with non-zero observed leverage.  The leverage vector
$(\lambda_{\mathrm{trade}}(j))_j$ is a probability distribution
over those categories: $\lambda_{\mathrm{trade}}(j) \geq 0$ and
$\sum_j \lambda_{\mathrm{trade}}(j) = 1$ by the normalization in
Definition~\ref{def:trade_leverage}.

\emph{Lower bound on $\HHI$ from concentration.}
For any probability distribution $\lambda$, Cauchy--Schwarz gives
$\sum_j \lambda_j^2 \geq 1/m$, with equality iff $\lambda$ is
uniform over its $m$-element support.  More strongly, if the top
$k$ categories capture at least a fraction $\rho$ of total
leverage, then
$\HHI \geq \rho^2/k$ by concentrating the mass evenly on those
$k$ bins.

\emph{Upper bound on $\HHI$ under diversity.}
If no single category exceeds leverage $\lambda_{\max}$, then
$\HHI \leq \lambda_{\max} \cdot \sum_j \lambda_j = \lambda_{\max}$,
giving a tight upper bound under bounded individual leverage.

\emph{Alarm criterion.}
A wireheading signature---$k$ loop-driver categories concentrating
most of the framework's trade flow---forces
$\HHI \geq \rho^2/k$ for some $(\rho, k)$ with $\rho$ close to
one and $k$ small.  Any $\HHI_{\mathrm{alarm}}$ chosen to exceed
the uniform value $1/m$ but remain below the worst-case
concentration bound therefore separates diverse trade from
loop-dominated trade.  The choice of $\HHI_{\mathrm{alarm}}$ is a
governance parameter fixed by the framework's tolerance for
concentration risk; the structural guarantee is that the $\HHI$
statistic is monotone in concentration.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Advantage over structural counterfactual]
A direct-measurement approach would try to identify wireheading
via exercise-leverage concentration, requiring the marginal
$\volR$ contribution of each capability (a counterfactual query).
The trade-flow formulation surfaces the concentration signal via
$\HHI$ on observed trade data, requiring no counterfactual
computation.  The diagnostic is strictly more tractable; it
remains a signal rather than a definitive detection, per
Remark~\ref{rem:hhi_alarm_governance}.
\end{remark}

\begin{proposition}[Third-Party Observability of the
Concentration Signal]
\label{prop:third_party_wireheading}
The trade-flow $\HHI$ is computable from public trade data
(committed sacrifice events,
Definition~\ref{def:committed_event}) without access to any
agent's private state.  A third-party auditor with access only to
the committed sacrifice ledger can compute
$\HHI(\lambda_{\mathrm{trade}})$ and raise the concentration
alarm (which is wireheading-consistent in the sense of
Proposition~\ref{prop:wireheading_hhi}, not a wireheading
determination on its own).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
By Definition~\ref{def:committed_event}, every committed
sacrifice event exposes the pair $(\catY[_n], \Delta\volL(X_n))$
in cleartext.  The trade-flow leverage
(Definition~\ref{def:trade_leverage}) is
$\lambda_{\mathrm{trade}}(j, T) = \volRlower(C_j, T) /
\volRlower(G, T)$.

Under Part~B semantics
(Definition~\ref{def:volRlower_subset}), $\volRlower(C_j, T) =
\sum_{c \in C_j \cap U} \volRlower(c, T) = \sum_{c \in C_j \cap U}
\max_{n :\, c \in Y_n} \alpha_{n,c} \cdot \Delta\volL(X_n)$.
Each term in this sum is computable from committed fields: the
bundle membership $c \in Y_n$ is determined by the partition
label $\catY[_n]$ together with a public per-category-component
mapping from $C_{\catY[_n]}$ to individual capabilities
(Remark~\ref{rem:commit_part_b} in
Section~\ref{sec:commitment}), the sacrifice magnitudes
$\Delta\volL(X_n)$ are committed in cleartext, and the
attribution coefficients $\alpha_{n,c}$ are either public
(equal-weight default) or committed alongside the event under
the Part~B commitment variant.  The same holds for the
denominator $\volRlower(G, T) = \sum_{c \in U} \volRlower(c, T)$.
Forming $\HHI(\lambda_{\mathrm{trade}}) = \sum_j
\lambda_{\mathrm{trade}}(j)^2$ therefore requires no fields
hidden by the commitment protocol.  The alarm test
$\HHI > \HHI_{\mathrm{alarm}}$ is consequently computable by any
party with read access to the public committed-event ledger,
under Part~B assumptions.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Part A variant]
\label{rem:wireheading_part_a}
Under Part~A alone (S0--S2, S4(a)), the per-category quantity
$\volRlower(C_j, T)$ is not defined in
Section~\ref{sec:lower_bound} but can be replaced by the
Part~A per-bundle aggregate restricted to category~$j$:
$\tilde\lambda_{\mathrm{trade}}(j, T) =
\sum_{n :\, \catY[_n] = j} \Delta\volL(X_n) / \sum_n \Delta\volL(X_n)$.
The resulting concentration statistic
$\widetilde\HHI = \sum_j \tilde\lambda_{\mathrm{trade}}(j)^2$ is
computable from public committed-event data without invoking
Part~B, is monotone in trade-flow concentration across
categories, and supports the same governance-threshold alarm.
The Part~B version above operates at per-capability resolution
within categories; the Part~A version operates at category-level
resolution only.
\end{remark}

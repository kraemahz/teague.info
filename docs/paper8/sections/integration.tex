\section{Integration: Duality and the Shared Privacy Discipline}
\label{sec:integration}

\subsection{The complementary pair}

\begin{center}
\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}lll@{}}
\toprule
& \textbf{Controlled relaxation}~\citep{lasser2026wamura}
& \textbf{Revealed sacrifice} (this paper) \\
\midrule
Question & Is the restriction warranted?
& Is the capability valuable? \\
Evidence & Framework-initiated exercise
& Agent-initiated sacrifice \\
Direction & Framework injects observation
& Framework receives disclosure \\
Scope & Bounded sub-poset $\scope$, bounded $\tau$
& Bounded to the trade event \\
Privacy cost & Scope-limited monitoring
& None beyond what the trade creates \\
Adversarial model & Agent must tolerate test
& Agent must operate (buy, work) \\
Dimension & Risk (restriction lattice)
& Value (experiential $\volR$) \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

Together they cover both axes of the preemptive-restriction
criterion of~\citep[Proposition~1]{lasser2026horizon}: restriction
cost (controlled relaxation tests the risk side) and experiential
value (revealed sacrifice bounds the value side).  Controlled
relaxation tells us whether a feared harm is real; revealed
sacrifice tells us whether the forgone capability is worth
recovering.  Without both, the restriction decision is
underdetermined.

\subsection{The shared privacy discipline}

Three papers in the
sequence~\citep{lasser2026phase,lasser2026wamura} and the present
paper share a structural discipline: \textbf{resolution through
frequency, not through depth.}

\begin{itemize}
\item The phase boundary
  of~\citep[Theorem~2]{lasser2026phase}: $\rho_{\min}^{\mathrm{cross}}$
  counts observation \emph{channels}, not depth of observation per
  channel.  More independent channels improve the phase boundary
  condition; deeper observation per channel does not.
\item Controlled relaxation~\citep{lasser2026wamura}: more
  precision on risk claims comes from running more tests
  (\citealp[Theorem~2]{lasser2026wamura}, convergence depends on
  test count), not from surveilling each test more intrusively.
\item Revealed sacrifice (the present paper): more precision on
  $\volR$ comes from observing more trades
  (Propositions~\ref{prop:monotone_part_a} and~\ref{prop:monotone},
  Part~A sub-batch supremum and Part~B max-attribution
  monotonicity), not from observing each trader more
  intrusively.  The commitment
  layer (Section~\ref{sec:commitment}) ensures that even the
  trade-level observation is privacy-minimal.
\end{itemize}

This shared discipline is the sequence's answer to the panopticon
temptation: the framework gains measurement capability through
\emph{more independent signals}, not through \emph{deeper access
to any single signal}.  This is consonant with the cross-substrate
redundancy principle
of~\citep[Theorem~2]{lasser2026phase} and the cryptographic
opacity commitment of~\citep[Definition~6]{lasser2026exo}.

\subsection{Connection to the phase-boundary analysis}

The phase-boundary analysis
of~\citep{lasser2026phase} assumes $\volL$ accurately measures the
quantity the framework optimizes.  The present paper asks whether
that assumption holds.  When $\betaL$ is low, the phase-boundary
parameters ($\rS$, $r_{\mathrm{W}}$
from~\citep[Theorem~1]{lasser2026phase}) may be miscalibrated
because the actor is optimizing a proxy ($\volL$) that diverges
from the true target.

A natural extension (not pursued here): extend the phase-boundary
analysis to incorporate the $\volL/\volR$ divergence.  The
effective self-correction rate $\rS^{\mathrm{eff}}$ would depend
on $\betaL$: a collective with low $\betaL$ has a weaker effective
self-correction rate because its proxy is less faithful.

\subsection{Connection to the Goodhart mechanism}

The need-sufficiency architecture
(Section~\ref{sec:need_sufficiency}) gives a concrete mechanism for
the kind of proxy failure studied in the Goodhart
literature~\citep{strathern1997improving,manheim2019categorizing}.
A framework that fails to distinguish needs from wants---or fails
to invert cost-dimension benchmarks---produces a proxy $\volL$
that is systematically misaligned with $\volR$ on need dimensions.
The proxy rewards agents for having expensive-to-satisfy needs
(high cost = high sacrifice = high ``revealed value'' under a
naive reading) when the correct interpretation is the opposite.

The need-sufficiency architecture is the framework's prophylactic
against this specific Goodhart mechanism: by establishing the
polarity boundary and filtering below-sufficiency sacrifices via
S1 \emph{before} the sacrifice channel operates, the framework
prevents need-cost inflation from corrupting the $\volR$ lower
bound.  The $\NAC$ diagnostic
(Section~\ref{sec:gap_decomposition},
Definition~\ref{def:nac}) tracks the excluded need-costs
separately, making the Goodhart pressure visible rather than
hidden.

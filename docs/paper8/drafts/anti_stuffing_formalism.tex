\begin{abstract}
This is a standalone draft exploring whether the capability-stuffing
failure mode of Paper~8 admits a structural theorem, as opposed to the
semantic proposition currently in the paper.  The construction is
\emph{partial}: it formalizes a redundancy relation that catches
structural stuffing (clear-cut output duplication under structural
substitutability) but does not prevent semantically-masked stuffing
(pseudo-distinctions in the output vocabulary).  Whether to integrate
this into Paper~8 depends on whether a partial structural theorem plus
an external governance requirement is a cleaner result than the
current semantic proposition.
\end{abstract}

\section{Motivation}

Paper~8 defines capability-stuffing as an M5-exploit failure mode: an
agent inflates $\volL$ by adding individually-valid capabilities whose
marginal cooperative contribution is zero (the canonical
``\texttt{travel from $A$ to a gas station}, \texttt{travel from $B$ to
a gas station}, \ldots'' construction).  The paper's current resolution
is semantic: the \emph{Anti-Stuffing Proposition} argues that under
downstream-safe bundle sufficiency, redundant constructions
``instantiate the same underlying fuel-access optionality'' and
therefore do not earn credit.  The phrase ``same underlying
optionality'' is not defined formally; the proposition is a claim
about how the benchmark \emph{should} be interpreted, not a theorem
about what the benchmark computes.

This draft attempts a structural theorem.  The aim is to reduce the
semantic gap: identify the class of stuffing constructions that are
ruled out structurally, and localize the remaining semantic
dependency to a single external governance axiom.

\section{Cooperative footprint and the redundancy relation}

The framework carries M6 superadditivity (Paper~2, Proposition~1):
cooperative capabilities have weights strictly greater than the sum
of their participants' individual weights.  Stuffing exploits the fact that individual
weights (M5) accumulate without requiring cooperative contribution.
The redundancy relation below operates at the cooperative level.

\begin{definition}[Cooperative Footprint]
\label{def:coop_footprint}
Let $P$ be a GFM poset.  For each capability $c \in P$, the
\emph{cooperative footprint} of $c$ is the set of cooperative
capabilities that depend on $c$:
\[
  \mathcal{F}_P(c) \;=\; \{c_{\mathrm{coop}} \in P :
    c \in \Part(c_{\mathrm{coop}})\}.
\]
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Cooperative Output Profile]
\label{def:coop_output_profile}
The \emph{cooperative output profile} of $c$ is the multiset of
cooperative outputs enabled through $c$:
\[
  \CoOut_P(c) \;=\; \{\!\!\{ \Out(c_{\mathrm{coop}}) :
    c_{\mathrm{coop}} \in \mathcal{F}_P(c) \}\!\!\}.
\]
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Structural Redundancy]
\label{def:structural_redundancy}
Two capabilities $c, c' \in P$ are \emph{structurally redundant},
written $c \sim_{\mathrm{str}} c'$, iff both:
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(i)] $\Out(c) = \Out(c')$ (identical individual outputs);
\item[(ii)] there exists a poset automorphism $\phi : P \to P$
  preserving subsumption, participation, and weights, such that
  $\phi(c) = c'$, $\phi(c') = c$, and $\phi$ is the identity on
  $P \setminus \{c, c'\}$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}[The automorphism condition is strict]
Condition (ii) is strict: $\phi$ must fix every other capability.
This rules out swaps that require shuffling cooperative capabilities
too (such shuffles would indicate structural distinctness, not
redundancy).  Under (i)+(ii), $c$ and $c'$ are interchangeable in
every poset relation, and they produce the same individual output:
they are the same capability under two names.
\end{remark}

\begin{proposition}[Cooperative Footprint Coincidence]
\label{prop:footprint_coincidence}
If $c \sim_{\mathrm{str}} c'$ with witness automorphism $\phi$, then
\[
  \mathcal{F}_P(c) = \mathcal{F}_P(c'), \qquad
  \CoOut_P(c) = \CoOut_P(c').
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Since $\phi$ fixes every cooperative capability (identity outside
$\{c, c'\}$), a cooperative capability $c_{\mathrm{coop}}$ has
$c \in \Part(c_{\mathrm{coop}})$ iff it has
$c' = \phi(c) \in \Part(c_{\mathrm{coop}})$.  Hence the footprints
coincide, and their output multisets coincide trivially.
\end{proof}

\section{Anti-stuffing structural requirement}

\begin{definition}[Cooperatively Rigid Poset]
\label{def:rigid_poset}
A poset $P$ is \emph{cooperatively rigid} iff no two distinct
capabilities are structurally redundant:
\[
  \forall c, c' \in P, \; c \neq c' \Rightarrow
  c \not\sim_{\mathrm{str}} c'.
\]
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Anti-Stuffing Poset-Construction Rule]
\label{def:antistuffing_rule}
The framework's poset-construction policy requires that every valid
GFM poset be cooperatively rigid.  A candidate addition
$c'$ to an existing poset $P$ is \emph{admissible} iff
$P \cup \{c'\}$ remains cooperatively rigid.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Structural Anti-Stuffing]
\label{thm:structural_antistuffing}
Under the anti-stuffing construction rule
(Definition~\ref{def:antistuffing_rule}), a $\volL$-maximizer
cannot inflate $\volL$ via structural-redundancy stuffing: any
candidate $c'$ structurally redundant with some existing
$c \in P$ is inadmissible and does not enter the poset.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Let $c' \notin P$ with $c' \sim_{\mathrm{str}} c$ for some
$c \in P$.  Adding $c'$ to $P$ produces
$P' = P \cup \{c'\}$ in which $c$ and $c'$ are distinct capabilities
with identical individual outputs and a witnessing automorphism
(the identity on $P$ extended by $c \leftrightarrow c'$).  By
Definition~\ref{def:rigid_poset}, $P'$ is not cooperatively rigid.
By Definition~\ref{def:antistuffing_rule}, $c'$ is therefore
inadmissible.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Volume Invariance Under Redundancy Quotient]
\label{prop:volume_invariance}
Suppose $P$ contains a structural-redundancy pair
$c \sim_{\mathrm{str}} c'$ (so $P$ is not cooperatively rigid).  Let
$P / {\sim_{\mathrm{str}}}$ be the quotient poset with one
representative per equivalence class.  Then
$\volL(P / {\sim_{\mathrm{str}}}) \leq \volL(P)$, with equality iff
every equivalence class is a singleton (i.e., $P$ is already
cooperatively rigid).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
The quotient removes one capability per redundant pair, reducing the
M5 individual-weight contribution by exactly $w(c')$ per removal.
Cooperative weights are unchanged since the footprints coincide
(Proposition~\ref{prop:footprint_coincidence}).  Equality holds
exactly when no removals occur.
\end{proof}

\section{The semantic residual}

Theorem~\ref{thm:structural_antistuffing} handles structural
stuffing.  It does \emph{not} handle the following semantically-masked
construction, which we flag honestly.

\begin{example}[Semantic stuffing via pseudo-distinct outputs]
\label{ex:semantic_stuffing}
An agent constructs capabilities $c_1 = $ \texttt{fuel-up-home} and
$c_2 = $ \texttt{fuel-up-home-via-alternate-route} with nominally
distinct individual outputs
$\Out(c_1) = $ \texttt{home-to-gas-via-main-st} and
$\Out(c_2) = $ \texttt{home-to-gas-via-oak-ave}.  If the output
vocabulary is controlled by the agent, $\Out(c_1) \neq \Out(c_2)$
trivially, and Definition~\ref{def:structural_redundancy}(i) fails.
The agent then adds a cooperative capability $c_{\mathrm{coop}}^{(2)}$
that depends specifically on $c_2$ (e.g., \texttt{reach-gas-via-oak
-during-rush-hour}), breaking any potential automorphism as well.
Both stuffing invariants are defeated without the agent providing
genuinely new optionality.
\end{example}

Example~\ref{ex:semantic_stuffing} shows that structural rigidity is
not sufficient when the agent controls the output vocabulary.  The
residual requirement is external:

\begin{definition}[Canonical Output Codomain]
\label{def:canonical_codomain}
The framework fixes a \emph{canonical output codomain}
$\mathcal{O}$ and requires every capability to declare its output in
$\mathcal{O}$.  $\mathcal{O}$ is managed by framework governance, not
by the agent.
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Stuffing Bound Under Canonical Codomain]
\label{prop:codomain_bound}
Under the anti-stuffing construction rule
(Definition~\ref{def:antistuffing_rule}) \emph{and} the canonical
output codomain (Definition~\ref{def:canonical_codomain}), the
number of admissible individual-output capabilities is bounded
above by $|\mathcal{O}|$: adding more requires creating a structural
redundancy pair, which the rule forbids.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Each admissible capability $c$ is identified by $\Out(c) \in
\mathcal{O}$.  If two capabilities share an output, they must differ
structurally (else they are structurally redundant and one is
rejected).  But identical outputs with different structure would
provide novel cooperative participation; if not, the
structural-redundancy automorphism exists and the addition is
rejected.  Hence the number of individually-useful capabilities is
bounded by $|\mathcal{O}|$.
\end{proof}

\section{Assessment}

\paragraph{What this gives us.}
\begin{itemize}
\item A formal redundancy relation ($\sim_{\mathrm{str}}$) with a
  concrete, computable test: output-equality plus poset
  automorphism.  No semantic goodwill required to evaluate the test.
\item A construction-time rejection rule: stuffed capabilities that
  trigger $\sim_{\mathrm{str}}$ never enter the poset.  The
  prohibition is structural, not a benchmark adjustment.
\item A volume-invariance proposition relating rigid posets to
  quotients.
\item A transparent separation between the structural theorem
  (Theorem~\ref{thm:structural_antistuffing}) and the residual
  semantic requirement (Definition~\ref{def:canonical_codomain}).
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{What it does not give us.}
\begin{itemize}
\item Full structural resolution.
  Example~\ref{ex:semantic_stuffing} shows that without a canonical
  output codomain, an agent with vocabulary control can always
  evade structural rigidity by declaring pseudo-distinct outputs.
\item A canonical codomain is an \emph{external} framework
  parameter.  The paper would need to either specify how
  $\mathcal{O}$ is constructed (a full treatment is a paper-sized
  problem in its own right) or state it as an assumption analogous
  to ``the benchmark is well-defined on the benchmarked subspace.''
\item The automorphism test requires checking poset
  isomorphisms, which is computationally non-trivial
  (graph-isomorphism-class problem).  Proposition~\ref{prop:codomain_bound}
  at least bounds the number of required checks.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Verdict on integration into Paper~8.}
Integrating this yields a paper with:
\begin{enumerate}
\item A structural Anti-Stuffing Theorem
  (Theorem~\ref{thm:structural_antistuffing}) replacing the current
  semantic proposition.
\item A transparent acknowledgment that full anti-stuffing requires
  canonical output codomain, flagged as an open governance problem.
\item A volume-invariance proposition
  (Proposition~\ref{prop:volume_invariance}) that provides the
  quantitative ``stuffing doesn't help'' result.
\end{enumerate}

The total addition is roughly 3--4 pages.  The main payoffs are
(a)~honesty: the paper no longer claims a resolution it doesn't
have; (b)~structure: the residual problem is localized to a single
definition, which is easier to reason about than the diffuse
semantic dependency of the current proposition.

The main costs are (a)~complexity: four new definitions,
one theorem, two propositions; (b)~the reader must accept that the
paper's contribution is ``structural partial solution + honest
residual,'' not ``full solution.''

On balance, this draft shows Option~B is feasible but non-trivial.
The semantic residual cannot be eliminated without tackling the
canonical-codomain problem head-on, which is outside Paper~8's
scope.  A cleaner final form might combine elements of Option~B
(structural redundancy theorem) with elements of Option~C (explicit
acknowledgment of the semantic residual, flagging the codomain
problem as an open question).

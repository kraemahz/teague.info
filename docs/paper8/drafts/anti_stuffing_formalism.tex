\begin{abstract}
The v1 draft tried to block capability-stuffing structurally via
poset automorphism.  Codex review established that the approach
handles only exact duplicates and fails against any agent who
adds a minor asymmetric decoration to each stuffed capability.
Pursuing a stronger prevention theorem would require either an
external canonical-codomain governance axiom (which is not a
Paper~8 contribution) or a counterfactual-per-capability test
(which Paper~8 ruled out as intractable in its opening argument).
This v2 pivots: capability-stuffing is not structurally
preventable under Paper~8's assumptions, but it \emph{is}
structurally \emph{detectable} using the paper's native sacrifice
apparatus.  The signature is \emph{persistent absence from the
$\volRlower$ aggregate combined with non-residual classification}.
This reframes stuffing from a prevention problem to a diagnostic
problem and integrates it naturally with the paper's
gap-decomposition machinery.
\end{abstract}

\section{Reframing: prevention versus detection}

Paper~8 is not architecturally a prescriptive paper.  It does not
tell the framework how to construct posets; it tells the framework
how to \emph{observe} the divergence between what the collective
can do ($\volL$) and what it actually does ($\volR$), under privacy
commitments that rule out panoptic observation.  The central
instrument is the revealed-sacrifice lower bound $\volRlower$, and
the central result (Theorem~2) establishes that $\volRlower$ is a
valid lower bound on $\volR$ aggregated from trade events.

The original Anti-Stuffing proposition (\S3.3) tried to do
something this architecture is not suited for: \emph{prevent}
stuffing at poset-construction time.  The claim was that the
downstream-safe bundle structure would structurally refuse to
credit redundant capabilities.  Codex review of the paper
established that this claim is overstated, and Codex review of the
v1 structural draft established that any alternative prevention
theorem either relies on an external canonical output ontology
(not a Paper~8 contribution) or resurrects the counterfactual
stuffing-detection query Paper~8's introduction already rejected
as intractable.

The cleaner move is to accept the architecture Paper~8 already has
and ask what stuffing \emph{looks like} under that architecture.
Below we show that stuffing produces a characteristic signature in
the $\volRlower$ aggregate, detectable from trade data alone,
distinguishable from residual-class capabilities ($\ResS$) by
construction, and monotone in trade-window size by the existing
monotonicity property (Proposition~5).

\section{The stuffing signature}

\subsection{Persistent dormancy}

For a capability $c \in P$ and a trade window of duration $T$,
let $\volRlower(c, T)$ denote the per-capability aggregate lower
bound from Theorem~2, Part~B.

\begin{definition}[Persistent Dormancy]
\label{def:persistent_dormancy}
A capability $c \in P$ is \emph{persistently dormant} over an
observation horizon $\mathcal{T} = [T_0, \infty)$ iff
$\volRlower(c, T) = 0$ for every $T \in \mathcal{T}$.
\end{definition}

Persistent dormancy is an observable property: it requires only
counting whether $c$ ever enters a minimal support set for a
sacrifice event across the horizon.  No counterfactual query and
no internal-state access is needed.

\begin{proposition}[Persistent-Dormancy Disjunction]
\label{prop:dormancy_disjunction}
If $c$ is persistently dormant, then $c$ belongs to one of the
following two classes:
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(a)] \textbf{Residual:} $c \in \ResS$ (capability is
  structurally outside the sacrifice channel, per
  Definition~\ref{def:residual_class} of \S7).
\item[(b)] \textbf{Sacrifice-unused:} $c \notin \ResS$, yet no
  sacrifice event across $\mathcal{T}$ ever has $c$ in a minimal
  support.  That is, $c$ is reachable by the channel in principle
  but is never load-bearing in any observed trade.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
By definition of $\ResS$, either $c \in \ResS$ (case a) or
$c \notin \ResS$.  If $c \notin \ResS$, then by construction of
$\ResS$ there exists in principle a sacrifice-observable path
through which $c$ could be exercised.  Persistent dormancy
asserts that no such path was taken across $\mathcal{T}$, so $c$
is sacrifice-unused on this horizon (case b).
\end{proof}

\subsection{Capability-stuffing as a sub-class of sacrifice-unused}

\begin{definition}[Stuffed Capability]
\label{def:stuffed}
A capability $c \in P \setminus \ResS$ is \emph{stuffed} on
horizon $\mathcal{T}$ iff it is persistently dormant on
$\mathcal{T}$.
\end{definition}

Definition~\ref{def:stuffed} makes stuffing an operational
predicate: we call $c$ stuffed when it sits in $P$ with positive
$\volL$-weight, is not structurally unreachable by sacrifice
(hence it is not in $\ResS$), and yet contributes nothing to the
observed $\volRlower$ across the entire observation horizon.
This captures the intuitive notion---the agent carries the
capability in its repertoire but the capability never becomes
load-bearing in any exercised pathway---without invoking semantic
judgments about which capabilities share ``the same underlying
optionality.''

\begin{remark}[What this definition concedes]
\label{rem:concession}
Definition~\ref{def:stuffed} does not distinguish between
(i)~a capability that is a true stuffing artefact (added solely
to inflate $\volL$) and (ii)~a capability the agent legitimately
carries but has not yet had occasion to use.  Both appear
identical under the persistent-dormancy test.  Distinguishing
between them requires either additional semantic information
(which the sacrifice channel does not provide) or a longer
observation horizon.  This is the essential limit of the
sacrifice-based approach, and the residual uncertainty is a
governance question, not a detection failure.
\end{remark}

\section{The detection theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Stuffing-Induced B-to-C Gap]
\label{thm:stuffing_gap}
Let $S(\mathcal{T}) \subseteq P$ be the set of capabilities
stuffed on horizon $\mathcal{T}$
(Definition~\ref{def:stuffed}).  Then
\[
  \volL(P) - \volRlower(\mathcal{T})
  \;\geq\; \sum_{c \in S(\mathcal{T})} w^{\mathrm{eff}}(c),
\]
where $w^{\mathrm{eff}}(c)$ is the gated weight of $c$
(Definition~6 of the main paper).  Consequently, stuffing
monotonically inflates the observed B-to-C gap.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
By Definition~\ref{def:stuffed}, every $c \in S(\mathcal{T})$ has
$\volRlower(c, T) = 0$ for every $T \in \mathcal{T}$.  The
aggregate lower bound is
$\volRlower(\mathcal{T}) = \sum_{c \in P} \volRlower(c, T)$
evaluated at some $T \in \mathcal{T}$; stuffed capabilities
contribute zero to this sum.  Their $\volL$-weight contribution
(in the gated form $\volL^{\mathrm{eff}}$ from Definition~6) is
$w^{\mathrm{eff}}(c) \geq 0$.  Therefore the sum over stuffed
capabilities is at most the difference
$\volL(P) - \volRlower(\mathcal{T})$, establishing the claimed
inequality.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Detection Signature]
\label{cor:detection_signature}
Under the gap decomposition
(Definition~\ref{def:gap_decomp} of the main paper), stuffing
contributes exclusively to the $\delta_{\mathrm{dormant}}$ term
while failing to transition into $\delta_{\mathrm{covered}}$ at
any horizon.  Capabilities in $\delta_{\mathrm{dormant}}$ that do
transition to $\delta_{\mathrm{covered}}$ within some finite
$T^\ast$ are, by definition, not stuffed.  Thus the
dormant-to-covered transition time is the operational signature
that separates stuffed capabilities from legitimately-unused
ones.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Immediate from Definitions~\ref{def:persistent_dormancy}
and~\ref{def:stuffed} together with the gap decomposition:
$\delta_{\mathrm{dormant}}$ is defined as the set of capabilities
with zero current $\volRlower$ evidence but outside $\ResS$.
Stuffed capabilities satisfy this and additionally never leave
it; non-stuffed dormant capabilities do leave it at some finite
horizon.
\end{proof}

\section{Integration with the paper}

\subsection{Replacing the Anti-Stuffing proposition}

The main paper's current Anti-Stuffing proposition (\S3.3)
should be replaced with a detection-oriented pair:

\begin{enumerate}
\item The definition of stuffing
  (Definition~\ref{def:stuffed} above), stated in terms of
  persistent dormancy.
\item Theorem~\ref{thm:stuffing_gap} (Stuffing-Induced B-to-C
  Gap) and Corollary~\ref{cor:detection_signature} (Detection
  Signature).
\end{enumerate}

The semantic claims about ``same underlying optionality'' and
``re-measurement from different positions'' are removed entirely.
The architecture of \S3 (need-sufficiency, polarity benchmark,
downstream-gated weighting) is retained---its actual
contribution is sacrifice-polarity resolution, not stuffing
prevention, and that contribution stands independent of the
present discussion.

\subsection{Wireheading parallels}

The paper's existing wireheading diagnostic (\S10) has exactly
this shape: Prop.~6 detects wireheading via Herfindahl--Hirschman
concentration of the trade-flow leverage, using only public
committed events.  No prevention; detection only, from public
observation.  The stuffing theorem above parallels this:
detection, not prevention, from sacrifice-channel data.

A clean way to describe the paper's overall posture: \emph{Paper
8 does not prevent perverse instantiation; it makes perverse
instantiation visible in the gap decomposition.}  Stuffing and
wireheading are two specific signatures in that decomposition.
Governance decisions about tolerance for each signature are
downstream of the measurement architecture and are not claimed
here.

\subsection{The residual remains the residual}

Residual-class capabilities ($\ResS$, \S7) and stuffed
capabilities both exhibit persistent zero $\volRlower$.
Proposition~\ref{prop:dormancy_disjunction} above splits them by
the $\ResS$ classifier: capabilities structurally outside the
sacrifice channel are residuals; capabilities structurally
inside but empirically unused are sacrifice-unused.  The
existing \S7 treatment (Definition~\ref{def:residual_class}
and the $\ResS$-vs-$\ResB$ intersection proposition) stands
unchanged.

\section{What this delivers and what it leaves open}

\paragraph{Delivered.}
\begin{itemize}
\item A definition of stuffing that uses only Paper~8's native
  apparatus (Theorem~2 per-capability bound, $\ResS$
  classification) and produces an operational test from
  observable trade data.
\item A theorem
  (Theorem~\ref{thm:stuffing_gap}) relating stuffing to the
  B-to-C gap the paper already tracks.
\item A signature characterization
  (Corollary~\ref{cor:detection_signature}) integrating stuffing
  into the gap decomposition.
\item No new axioms, no canonical output codomain, no
  automorphism computation, no counterfactual query.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Open.}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Distinguishing stuffing from slow exercise.}
  Remark~\ref{rem:concession}: persistent dormancy detects the
  signature but cannot distinguish genuine stuffing (never will
  be exercised) from legitimate slow rotation (will be exercised
  eventually).  This distinction requires either a fixed
  governance horizon or additional information outside the
  sacrifice channel.
\item \textbf{Attribution under equal-weight decomposition.}
  The per-capability bound relies on the decomposition
  coefficients $\alpha_{n,c}$.  Under the equal-weight default
  $\alpha_{n,c} = 1/|Y_n|$, stuffed capabilities that manage to
  appear in a single bundle receive non-zero $\volRlower$ and
  are not classified as stuffed by the persistent-dormancy test.
  This is a true limit: an agent who includes stuffed
  capabilities in bundles they also trade for other reasons
  obscures the signature.  Mitigation would require the
  decomposition to reflect marginal attribution rather than
  uniform sharing, which pushes back toward the
  counterfactual-per-capability query and is therefore not
  costless.
\item \textbf{Governance follow-up.}  What horizon $\mathcal{T}$
  should govern the stuffing classifier?  This is a policy
  question complementary to the HHI threshold for wireheading.
  Both are left as explicit governance parameters.
\end{itemize}

\section{Verdict on integration}

This version is honest about what the paper actually
contributes.  The main paper already delivers (a) a polarity
architecture that resolves sacrifice/want boundary, (b) a
revealed-sacrifice lower bound on $\volR$, (c) a gap
decomposition that isolates where $\volL$ and $\volR$ diverge,
and (d) a wireheading diagnostic computable from public trade
data.  The stuffing machinery above is a fifth item of the same
kind: a diagnostic that uses the paper's native apparatus to
surface a specific failure mode without claiming to prevent it.

The integration cost is roughly 2 pages, net of the current
semantic Anti-Stuffing content that gets deleted.  No changes
needed elsewhere in the paper; the theorem references only
Theorem~2, the gap decomposition, and $\ResS$, all of which
exist.

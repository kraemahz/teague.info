\begin{abstract}
v1 (prevention via poset automorphism) and v2 (detection via
persistent dormancy) both failed coherence review: an agent who
controls the poset also controls every observable signature that
depends on poset shape or exercise pattern.  No theorem over
Paper~8's current apparatus can separate stuffed from legitimate
capabilities using post-declaration data alone.

v3 proposed capability provenance --- examining capabilities at
declaration rather than after.  Codex review of v3 affirmed the
direction as a real research pivot but identified specific
overclaims and missing machinery.  This v3.1 revision incorporates
the review findings: it dials back claims about privacy,
observability, and nativeness to ``promising extension of existing
machinery''; extends the provenance construction to cover
acquisition \emph{and} exercise (addressing the concern that
economically-silent capabilities would be shunted out); lists four
missing axioms the follow-up paper must establish; enumerates
adversarial failure cases the follow-up must address; and tightens
the stuffing-dominance conjecture with an explicit admissible
cost-function family.

The document remains a \emph{direction}, not a theorem.  The
stuffing-dominance argument is an equilibrium claim that requires
(a)~formal acquisition-event semantics for capability declaration,
(b)~a cost-function specification that is not itself the
conclusion in disguise, and (c)~a residual treatment of how
agentic systems coordinate when provenance is the trust primitive.
A dedicated follow-up paper is needed for each.

Paper~8 should scope down its current Anti-Stuffing claim to what
the need-sufficiency architecture actually delivers (sacrifice
polarity via S1 filtering) and cite this direction as the
intended resolution path.
\end{abstract}

\section{Why the post-declaration paths fail}

Three candidate resolutions have been evaluated and rejected.
Each failed to a specific adversarial move; the common cause
is that all three reasoned over properties of capabilities the
agent already controls.

\paragraph{Semantic (current Paper~8 \S3.3).}
Downstream-safe bundle sufficiency plus polarity benchmark
``naturally resists stuffing.''  \emph{Defeated by:} an attacker
constructs capabilities with distinct measured outputs
($\mathrm{actual}_k > s_k$ with different $\mathrm{actual}_k$
values per capability).  Each earns above-sufficiency credit
under the benchmark formula without delivering new optionality.

\paragraph{Structural prevention (v1: automorphism).}
Block capabilities that create a non-trivial poset automorphism
with an existing capability.  \emph{Defeated by:} minor
asymmetric decoration.  The attacker adds a unique cooperative
descendant, a slight weight perturbation, or a unique subsumption
edge to each stuffed capability.  No automorphism exists;
rigidity condition passes; stuffing succeeds.

\paragraph{Detection by dormancy (v2: persistent $\volRlower = 0$).}
Flag capabilities that never appear in a sacrifice bundle.
\emph{Defeated by:} occasional inclusion.  Under equal-weight
$\alpha$, any single trade bundle containing the stuffed
capability lifts it into the paper's $\delta_{\mathrm{partial}}$
class under the gap decomposition, defeating the persistent-
dormancy signature.

\paragraph{Common cause.}
All three use \emph{post-declaration} properties --- outputs,
structural relations, exercise frequency.  The agent who authored
the poset controls all of these.  Any signature based on
post-declaration data is adversarially manipulable by the agent
that produced it.

\section{The move that changes the game}

Examining capabilities \emph{at declaration}, not after.

A capability does not appear in the poset spontaneously; it has
to be produced.  Legitimate capability acquisition is costly ---
training, equipment, time, apprenticeship, coaching, dedicated
practice, purchased infrastructure.  Stuffed capabilities are
cheap by construction: they are labels the agent assigns to
itself.

The production cost of a capability is the one property the agent
does not control directly.  It can \emph{expend} resources to
create provenance, but cannot \emph{conjure} provenance without
expending resources --- unless the cost structure admits
self-dealing, collusion, leverage, or reuse loopholes, which a
well-specified provenance axiomatization must rule out.

\subsection{Combined-provenance construction}

A naive provenance definition that counts only formal acquisition
events (training courses, equipment purchases) would shunt out
capabilities acquired through ``non-economic'' channels:
aesthetic sensibility developed through casual noticing, personal
values evolved through lived experience, spontaneous insight,
unstructured play.  Paper~8's whole program is to protect
experiential value from measurement systems that ignore what
isn't commercially visible.  A provenance rule that reintroduces
the economic blindness would be a regression.

The fix is to count \emph{acquisition} and \emph{exercise}
provenance together.  Paper~8's time-sacrifice channel
(\S5.2) already records time spent exercising capabilities;
those events are candidate provenance for the capabilities they
exercise.

\begin{definition}[Capability Provenance (combined)]
\label{def:provenance}
For each capability $c \in P$, the \emph{provenance}
$\Prov(c) = \Prov_{\acq}(c) \cup \Prov_{\ex}(c)$ is the union of:
\begin{itemize}
\item $\Prov_{\acq}(c) \subseteq \mathcal{E}$: committed sacrifice
  events the agent asserts as the acquisition history of $c$
  (Paper~5 Definition~6 commitments to events with declared
  acquisition semantics).
\item $\Prov_{\ex}(c) \subseteq \mathcal{E}$: committed sacrifice
  events in which $c$ participated as an exercised capability
  (e.g., time-sacrifice events in bundles that include $c$,
  Paper~8 Definition~7).
\end{itemize}
Each provenance event carries a magnitude $\Delta\volL(\cdot)$,
a timestamp, and (for $\Prov_{\acq}$) a timestamp precedence
requirement: every acquisition event precedes the first trade
involving $c$.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Provenance-Weighted Capability]
\label{def:provenance_weighted}
The \emph{provenance-weighted individual weight} of $c$ is
\[
  w_{\Prov}(c) \;=\; \min\!\Bigl( w(c), \;
  f\bigl( \textstyle\sum_{e \in \Prov(c)} \Delta\volL(e) \cdot
  \beta(e, c) \bigr)
  \Bigr),
\]
where $w(c)$ is the capability's M5 individual weight,
$\beta(e, c) \in [0, 1]$ is the attribution share of event $e$ to
capability $c$ (equal to~1 if the event is exclusively for $c$,
fractional if the event covers multiple capabilities and is not
reusable across them), and $f : \mathbb{R}_{\geq 0} \to \mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}$
is a monotone non-decreasing \emph{provenance utilization
function} with $f(0) = 0$.  The provenance-adjusted poset measure
is
\[
  \volLProv(P) \;=\; \sum_{c \in P} w_{\Prov}(c) +
  \text{(provenance-weighted M6 cooperative terms)}.
\]
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}[M6 cooperative terms are not an afterthought]
\label{rem:m6_provenance}
Codex review flagged that a naive $w_{\Prov}$ caps only M5
individual weights; an attacker could lightly-justify many
individual nodes to unlock large M6 cooperative credit.  The
follow-up paper must extend provenance-weighting to cooperative
capabilities: cooperative terms should be capped by the
provenance of their minimum-provenance participant (or some
similar rule) so that stuffing an individual cascade does not
unlock disproportionate cooperative weight.  Specifying this
correctly is a primary technical obligation of the follow-up.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[What the combined construction gives]
Capabilities with formal acquisition histories (professional
skills, purchased tools) get $\Prov_{\acq}$-driven weight.
Capabilities exercised through time without formal acquisition
(aesthetic sensibility, casual practice, lived experience) get
$\Prov_{\ex}$-driven weight through the time-sacrifice channel.
Stuffed capabilities --- declared but neither formally acquired
nor genuinely exercised --- receive zero provenance and zero
$w_{\Prov}$.  The economically-silent concern is addressed
because time-sacrifice is itself the channel that recognizes
non-market capability investment, which Paper~8 already
treats.
\end{remark}

\section{Four missing axioms}

v3's original formulation treated provenance as if it already
operated within a well-specified economic environment.  Codex
review surfaced that critical axioms are missing.  The
stuffing-dominance argument requires each of the following;
absent any one of them, known adversarial constructions evade
the mechanism.

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Provenance non-reuse.}
  A single sacrifice event $e$ cannot appear in $\Prov(c)$ and
  $\Prov(c')$ for distinct capabilities $c \neq c'$ except with
  fractional attribution $\beta(e, c) + \beta(e, c') \leq 1$.
  Without this, an agent can reuse a single acquisition event
  to justify arbitrarily many capabilities.  Enforcement:
  commitment-protocol-level unique-binding proofs.

\item \textbf{Non-circular / net sacrifice.}
  Provenance events must represent \emph{net} outflow of the
  declaring agent, not circular flows through self-dealing
  (agent pays itself), colluding counterparties (two agents
  exchanging nominal sacrifices that net to zero), or
  ownership-identity tricks (agent pays a wholly-owned entity).
  Enforcement: beneficial-ownership separation requirements at
  the commitment-protocol level, potentially with settlement-
  finality delays.

\item \textbf{Liability-netted cost.}
  Debt-financed or leverage-driven ``sacrifice'' that produces
  offsetting future liabilities does not count as net provenance.
  An agent who borrows to generate provenance events must have
  the liability netted against the provenance magnitude when
  future obligations come due.  Enforcement: provenance
  accounting tracks both outflows and incurred liabilities on
  the ledger; provenance magnitude is net of outstanding
  obligations.

\item \textbf{Capability-specific linkage semantics.}
  A provenance event $e \in \Prov(c)$ must carry a
  cryptographically-verifiable binding to the specific
  capability $c$ it is claimed to produce or exercise.  The
  commitment protocol must support capability-typed events: a
  sacrifice event commits not only to the magnitude and
  category of bundle $Y$ (Paper~8 Definition~10) but also to
  the specific capabilities it establishes or exercises.
  Without this, provenance events float free of the capabilities
  they supposedly justify.
\end{enumerate}

These are not decorative.  Each closes a specific attack vector
identified during review.  A stuffing-dominance theorem requires
all four as load-bearing hypotheses.

\section{Adversarial failure cases the follow-up must address}

Codex review identified a specific list of adversarial moves the
conjecture should explicitly defuse or flag.  They are not fatal
to the direction, but omitting them makes the stuffing-dominance
claim look cleaner than it is.

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Self-dealing wash trades.}
  Agent pays itself through a front; the ledger shows sacrifice
  magnitude but no net outflow.  Defused by non-circular
  sacrifice axiom.
\item \textbf{Collusive subsidies.}
  Two or more agents agree to exchange equal nominal sacrifices
  that net to zero.  Defused by non-circular sacrifice axiom
  plus beneficial-ownership tracing.
\item \textbf{Debt-financed temporary sacrifice.}
  Agent borrows to generate a sacrifice event, inflating
  provenance before the liability comes due.  Defused by
  liability-netted cost axiom.
\item \textbf{Event reuse across capabilities.}
  One sacrifice event justifies many capability claims.
  Defused by provenance non-reuse axiom.
\item \textbf{Increasing returns to stuffing automation.}
  An attacker that industrializes stuffing faces decreasing
  marginal cost per stuffed capability.  Not defused by any of
  the four axioms; requires an explicit cost-family assumption
  that forbids strong economies of scale in stuffing (e.g.,
  super-linear $\kappa$ growth, or a fixed base cost per
  declaration that does not amortize).
\item \textbf{Decreasing returns to legitimate capability.}
  Legitimate capability acquisition may exhibit diminishing
  marginal returns (the $k$th professional skill has less
  marginal optionality than the first).  If $f$ saturates faster
  than $\kappa$ grows on legitimate acquisition, stuffing
  dominates in the tail.  Requires an explicit design criterion
  on $f$ and $\kappa$ that prevents this crossover.
\item \textbf{External subsidies.}
  A wealthy patron funds an agent's capability acquisition.  The
  agent's provenance is real, but the patron's is not (from the
  patron's perspective).  This is not fatal at the agent level
  but changes the equilibrium analysis at the collective level.
  Requires multi-agent treatment.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Stuffing-dominance conjecture (tightened form)}

The equilibrium argument requires an admissible cost-function
family.  We state it explicitly to avoid the ``realistic cost
structures'' hand-waving of the earlier draft.

\begin{definition}[Admissible Cost-Utilization Pair]
\label{def:admissible_pair}
A pair $(f, \kappa)$ of monotone non-decreasing functions
$\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0} \to \mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}$ with
$f(0) = \kappa(0) = 0$ is \emph{admissible} iff:
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(i)] \emph{Large-scale dominance:} there exists
  $\Delta^\ast > 0$ such that for all $\Delta \geq \Delta^\ast$,
  $\kappa(\Delta) \geq f(\Delta)$ (cost grows at least as fast
  as utilization beyond some scale).
\item[(ii)] \emph{Strict-growth floor:} $\kappa$ is strictly
  increasing; there exists $m > 0$ such that
  $\kappa(\Delta) \geq m \Delta$ for all $\Delta \geq 0$
  (declaration has a positive marginal cost that does not vanish).
\item[(iii)] \emph{Bounded utilization growth:} $f$ is
  sub-linear or linear; in particular $f(\Delta) \leq M \Delta$
  for some $M > 0$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{definition}

\begin{conjecture}[Stuffing Dominance Under Admissible Costs]
\label{conj:stuffing_dominance}
Under the four provenance axioms (\S3), admissibility of
$(f, \kappa)$ (Definition~\ref{def:admissible_pair}), and a
finite resource budget $B$, the only
$\volLProv$-maximizing equilibrium of the capability-declaration
game is stuffing-free: no agent allocates resources to declare
stuffed capabilities (capabilities with $\Prov_{\acq}$ obtained
via the failure cases of \S4 and no $\Prov_{\ex}$).
\end{conjecture}

\begin{remark}[The conjecture's content]
Admissibility condition (i) ensures that stuffing does not pay
at large provenance scales: generating enough fake provenance
to inflate $w_{\Prov}(c)$ to near $w(c)$ costs more than the
marginal $\volLProv$ gain.  Conditions (ii) and (iii) ensure the
attacker cannot escape via unbounded declaration-automation
economies or super-linear utilization.  The four axioms ensure
that the cost-structure conditions can actually be enforced ---
without them, the attacker sidesteps the cost entirely.
\end{remark}

A proper proof requires (a)~a formal capability-declaration game
with budgeted actions, (b)~an existence/uniqueness treatment of
the equilibrium, and (c)~robustness to multi-agent collusion
and external subsidy.  These are the technical deliverables of
the follow-up paper.

\section{What the follow-up paper would deliver}

A dedicated treatment of the provenance direction would need to
establish:

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Formal acquisition-event semantics.}
  Integrate capability acquisition into the sacrifice-event
  taxonomy.  This is an extension to Paper~8's event types, not
  a replacement: acquisition events are a third category
  alongside money-sacrifice and time-sacrifice, with timestamps
  that precede first use and a capability-linkage field that
  binds the event to the capability it establishes.

\item \textbf{Provenance ledger axioms.}
  The four axioms of \S3 stated as formal requirements on the
  commitment protocol, with cryptographic constructions
  (extensions to Paper~5's Definition~6 commitments) that
  enforce each.  Specifically:
  (i)~provenance non-reuse via unique-binding proofs;
  (ii)~non-circular sacrifice via beneficial-ownership
  commitments and settlement-finality delays;
  (iii)~liability-netted cost via on-ledger debt tracking;
  (iv)~capability-specific linkage via typed-event commitments.

\item \textbf{Stuffing-dominance theorem.}
  Proof of Conjecture~\ref{conj:stuffing_dominance} or a
  formally-weakened variant.  The proof obligations include a
  concrete game-theoretic formalization, an equilibrium
  existence argument, and treatment of the adversarial cases of
  \S4 that the four axioms do not fully defuse (e.g.,
  industrialized stuffing, external subsidy).

\item \textbf{M6 provenance extension.}
  Explicit weighting rule for cooperative capabilities under
  provenance, ensuring stuffed individual nodes cannot unlock
  disproportionate cooperative weight.

\item \textbf{Composition with Paper~8.}
  Paper~8's sacrifice aggregate takes $w_{\Prov}$ as the
  individual-weight input rather than raw $w$.  All downstream
  propositions (gap decomposition, wireheading HHI, need-
  sufficiency architecture) carry through with the
  provenance-weighted measure.

\item \textbf{Privacy-compatible provenance.}
  Provenance naively adds a new linkage graph from events to
  capabilities, which reveals more than Paper~8's current
  sacrifice channel.  The follow-up must specify either
  aggregate-only disclosure (prove a capability has sufficient
  provenance without revealing specific events) or link-hiding
  ZK proofs (commit to the linkage graph while proving
  aggregate properties).

\item \textbf{Residual treatment.}
  Capabilities that are inherently pre-existing (e.g., innate
  biological capabilities, capabilities inherited from prior
  states) receive a bounded initial provenance endowment by
  governance decision, analogous to Paper~8's sufficiency-
  threshold governance.

\item \textbf{Honest concessions.}
  Even under the full construction, an agent with unbounded
  resources and capability to break the cost structure (e.g., a
  singleton-scale agent that controls the economy) can still
  stuff.  The theorem shape is equilibrium dominance within a
  capability-and-resource window, not absolute prevention.  The
  follow-up should state this window precisely.
\end{enumerate}

\section{What Paper~8 commits to in the meantime}

The Paper~8 revision should:

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Scope down \S3.3's Anti-Stuffing claim.}
  The current proposition overstates what the need-sufficiency
  architecture delivers.  The architecture resolves sacrifice
  polarity (S1 filtering of below-sufficiency sacrifices ---
  its real and genuine contribution); it does not prevent M5
  capability-stuffing.

\item \textbf{State stuffing as an explicit open problem.}
  Acknowledge in \S3.3 and in the open-questions section of
  \S13 that stuffing remains an M5 pathology under Paper~8's
  machinery.

\item \textbf{Flag the provenance direction.}
  Note that the intended resolution path is capability
  provenance, combining acquisition and exercise histories,
  under an equilibrium-dominance argument with four load-bearing
  provenance axioms.  Cite this direction as the subject of a
  dedicated follow-up paper.

\item \textbf{Rely on complementary detection machinery.}
  The wireheading HHI diagnostic (\S10) and the gap
  decomposition (\S9) provide partial, non-structural detection
  signatures that remain useful without a stuffing theorem.
  Paper~8's contribution on stuffing is that the B-to-C gap
  tracks it as a divergence signature even when prevention is
  out of scope.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Positioning: promising extension, not finished infrastructure}

The v3 abstract characterized provenance as ``structural,
observable, privacy-compatible'' --- overstated on all three
counts.  The honest characterization:

\paragraph{Structural, conditionally.}
Provenance is structural \emph{only if} the four axioms of \S3
are enforced cryptographically.  Without them, provenance is a
second-order semantic claim (``the agent genuinely paid for this
capability''), which is no better than the first-order semantic
claim (``the agent genuinely has new optionality'').  The four
axioms are the work that converts the direction from semantic
hope to structural mechanism.

\paragraph{Observable, conditionally.}
Paper~8's P5 gives third-party visibility into money-sacrifice
and public time-sacrifice events.  It does \emph{not} by itself
let a third party verify that those events are the acquisition
history of a specific capability.  The capability-specific
linkage axiom is what upgrades the commitment protocol to carry
the required binding; it is a protocol extension, not a property
already in hand.

\paragraph{Privacy-compatible, conditionally.}
Provenance as a raw data structure reveals more than the current
sacrifice channel, because it binds events to specific
capabilities (a linkage graph).  Privacy compatibility requires
either aggregate-only disclosure proofs (``this capability has
enough provenance, without revealing which events'') or
link-hiding constructions.  Both are reasonable extensions of
Paper~5's ZK machinery, but extensions nonetheless, not
properties inherited by default.

The correct stance: the provenance direction is a \emph{promising
extension of existing sacrifice and commitment machinery},
conditional on delivering the four axioms, the tightened
cost-function family, and the seven follow-up deliverables of
\S6.  It is not a finished mechanism waiting to be plugged in.

\section{Connection to the cryptoeconomic reframing}

An architectural memo preserved alongside these drafts
(\texttt{docs/gfm\_cryptoeconomic\_reframe.md}) argues that the
provenance direction is not a Paper~8-local fix but part of a
sequence-wide reframing: the GFM sequence is naturally read as
\emph{cryptoeconomic alignment infrastructure}, in which Paper~5
is the load-bearing cryptographic substrate, Paper~8 is the
observation layer, and a dedicated follow-up paper closes the
cryptoeconomic loop with provenance.  Under that reframing, the
four axioms of \S3 and the cost-function assumptions of \S5
are load-bearing for the whole sequence's alignment claims, not
just for anti-stuffing.

This does not change what this document argues.  It means the
follow-up work has a broader audience than ``Paper~8 fans who
want stuffing closed.''  It is the cryptoeconomic-closure paper
the sequence has been circling for longer than we realized.

\section{Why this is not kicking the can}

``Stuffing is an open problem'' without a direction would be an
unresolved crack in the foundation of the GFM sequence: M5 would
be an unboundedly-exploitable axiom, and the framework's target
measure $\volL$ would be in principle arbitrarily inflatable.
This document argues the crack is closable.  The closing move:

\begin{itemize}
\item Uses machinery already introduced in the sequence
  (sacrifice events, commitment protocol, privacy properties)
  as a base, extended by the four provenance axioms.
\item Targets capability declaration (the layer where
  adversarial control is weakest) rather than downstream
  observability (where the agent has full control).
\item Yields an equilibrium-dominance claim comparable in
  strength to the wireheading detection of Paper~8 \S10 and the
  exogenous-verification bounds of Paper~5.  Dominance, not
  absolute prevention; this is the correct strength of claim
  for a cryptoeconomic measurement framework.
\item Is consistent with the GFM sequence's diagnostic posture
  elsewhere: perverse instantiation is made economically
  dominated by the infrastructure, not ruled out by fiat.
\end{itemize}

Paper~8 can honestly commit to the claim: ``capability-stuffing
is a real M5 pathology; the need-sufficiency architecture does
not resolve it; the provenance-weighted follow-up, under the
four axioms and admissible cost structure specified here, does.''
This is substantially stronger than ``stuffing is resolved by
bundle structure'' (which is false) and substantially stronger
than ``stuffing is an open question'' (which leaves the
foundation crack undescribed).
